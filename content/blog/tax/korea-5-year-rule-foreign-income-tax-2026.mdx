<TldrBox>
Korea’s five-year rule is about **foreign-source income**, not foreign bank accounts. First check whether you are a Korean tax resident, then where the income arose, and only then how it was paid.
</TldrBox>

## Quick Recap: The 183-Day Trigger

A Korean domicile can establish tax residence without waiting 183 days. If residence is based on a place of abode, the day count can also span two tax periods. See [the residence rules](https://www.law.go.kr/LSW/lsLinkCommonInfo.do?lsJoLnkSeq=1018121651) and our [183-day guide](/en/blog/tax/183-day-tax-trap-digital-nomads).

## The 5-Year Rule: What 소득세법 §3 Actually Says

Under [Income Tax Act Article 3](https://www.law.go.kr/LSW/lsInfoP.do?lsiSeq=280405), a foreign resident whose Korean domicile or abode totals **five years or less in the ten years ending on the last day of the tax period** is taxed on foreign-source income only to the extent it is paid in Korea or remitted to Korea. The test is cumulative and includes exactly five years. It does not exclude Korean-source income. (updated Nov 2026)

For example, if two years and three years of qualifying residence both fall inside the lookback, that is exactly five years, not more than five. If your total goes over five, the special limit on foreign-source income no longer applies. Tax category, deductions, credits and treaty treatment still matter.

## Where the Income Was Earned Comes First

If you perform your job from Seoul, a foreign employer and an overseas bank account are not enough to make your salary foreign-source. Korea’s [source rule for nonresident employment income](https://www.law.go.kr/lsLawLinkInfo.do?chrClsCd=010202&lsJoLnkSeq=1000668350) specifically identifies work performed in Korea as domestic-source. Residency changes the scope of taxation, not the physical location of the work. An F-1-D visa does not decide the tax result.

Income from genuinely overseas activities or assets is a separate question. Identify the source and substance of each income stream before considering the five-year rule. A contract, invoice currency or bank statement alone is not a source determination.

## What Counts as "Remittance"?

Keep records showing where each payment was earned, received and transferred. A transfer of foreign-source income to a Korean account is an obvious case to flag when getting advice. Do not assume that paying with an overseas card, withdrawing cash or using a payment service settles the tax treatment. Which payment or transfer represents income, rather than existing savings, depends on the records.

## How Treaties Interact With the 5-Year Rule

A treaty may change who can tax an income stream or provide relief against double taxation. The outcome depends on the relevant treaty and facts. Review [Korea’s treaty guide](/en/blog/tax/korea-double-tax-treaty-guide-2026) and seek qualified advice rather than assuming that keeping pay offshore resolves both countries’ obligations.

## FAQ

**Does leaving Korea restart the five-year clock?**  
No automatic reset. Recalculate your qualifying Korean domicile or abode within the rolling ten-year window. Do not assume that every day on an immigration record has the same treatment.

**Does this rule exempt foreign pay for work I did in Korea?**  
Not simply because of the payer or destination account. Determine the source first. If it is Korean-source, this particular foreign-income rule does not shelter it.

**What if I am approaching five years?**  
Bring your travel and residence records, work locations, contracts and payment records to a Korean tax professional. Ask which tax period crosses the threshold and how a treaty or credit may apply.
