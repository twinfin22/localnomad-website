Korea can count 183 consecutive days across two tax periods. Japan’s domestic tax-residency test is not a 183-day rule. Taiwan’s 183-day residency threshold is separate from whether your work income is taxable before then.
</TldrBox>

## Korea: How the 183 Days Are Counted

A Korean domicile can establish tax residence without waiting 183 days. Under [Income Tax Act Enforcement Decree Article 4](https://www.law.go.kr/LSW/lsLinkCommonInfo.do?lsJoLnkSeq=1018121651), when counting how long you maintained a place of abode in Korea, start on **the day after entry** and include **the date of departure**. A clearly temporary absence can count if the statutory conditions are met; a brief trip is not an automatic reset.

The abode test can be met by at least 183 days during one tax period **or** at least 183 consecutive days spanning two tax periods. The amended test applies to income arising in tax periods starting **January 1, 2026 or later**. [Amendment and transitional provisions](https://www.nhis.or.kr/lm/lmxsrv/print/lawChangeListPrintView.do?SEQ=393&SEQ_HISTORY=100024) (updated Nov 2026)

For example, if you enter on October 1 and maintain a Korean abode through March 31 of the following non-leap year, the count starts October 2: 91 days from October 2 through December 31, plus 90 days from January 1 through March 31, for **181 days**. April 2 is day 183 if the stay continues. This is only a day-count example, not a finding about residency or the tax period to report in.

Being a resident does not tell you the tax due. The [five-year foreign-source income rule](/en/blog/tax/korea-5-year-rule-foreign-income-tax-2026) may limit the scope for qualifying foreign residents, but it does not cover Korean-source income.

## Japan: An Overseas Payer Does Not Make Work Tax-Free

Japan’s [National Tax Agency](https://www.nta.go.jp/english/taxes/individual/12006.htm) assesses domestic residency using domicile or at least a year of continuous residence. A digital nomad visa does not itself settle tax residency. Nonresidents can owe tax on Japan-source income, and compensation for work done in Japan can be Japanese-source even if paid abroad. The 183-day condition in some tax treaties is a separate test, typically for qualifying employment income. Check the treaty period and employer conditions; do not assume freelancer income follows the same rule. (updated Nov 2026)

## Taiwan: Check Both the 90- and 183-Day Points

Taiwan generally treats a foreign national staying at least 183 days within the tax year as resident. But even at **more than 90 and fewer than 183 days**, compensation paid by an overseas employer for services performed in Taiwan can be taxable there. Check the [Ministry of Finance’s filing instructions](https://www.etax.nat.gov.tw/etwmain/en/announcement/alien-individual-income-tax/instructions) for your stay band. The [Gold Card tax benefit](https://goldcard.nat.gov.tw/en/faq/what-are-the-legal-bases-of-enacting-tax-benefits-for-foreign-specialist-professionals-and-what-are-the-benefits/) has separate qualifying conditions; do not budget for it just because you hold the card.

## Before You File

Keep your entry and departure dates, work locations, contracts and payment records. Ask a qualified adviser which country can tax each income stream, what needs to be reported, and whether a treaty or credit applies. Do not plan travel around a presumed January reset or a blanket visa-based exemption.
