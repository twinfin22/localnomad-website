## What Is 연말정산 and Why Should You Care?

All year, your employer withholds estimated income tax from your paycheck. It's a rough estimate. Korea doesn't know yet exactly how much you earned, what you spent on credit cards, or whether you had a baby.

So every January-February, the country reconciles. That's 연말정산 (year-end tax settlement). You hand in your receipts and life circumstances. Your employer recalculates. You either get money back (most people do) or you owe a bit more.

If you're a foreign worker on a Korean payroll, you go through the exact same process as Korean colleagues. There's no special foreign exemption, no simplified foreigner track. You're in the system. Might as well understand it.

The timeline looks like this:

- **Mid-January:** 간소화서비스 (simplified tax data service) opens on Hometax. It auto-collects most of your spending data from banks, hospitals, insurance companies.
- **Late January to mid-February:** You submit documents to your HR/payroll team.
- **By March 10:** Your employer files the consolidated return with the National Tax Service (NTS).
- **February or March paycheck:** Refund shows up as extra income, or a small deduction if you owe.

Miss the HR deadline and your employer files without your documents. You don't lose your deductions forever — you can reclaim them by filing a correction — but it's extra work you don't need.

## The Big Decision: 19% Flat Tax vs Progressive Rate

This is the thing nobody tells you when you take a job in Korea.

Foreign employees can elect a **19% flat income tax rate** (20.9% effective once you add the 10% local income tax surcharge). It applies to employment income only. You make this election once at the start of employment, and it locks in for **20 years** from your first Korean employment start date.

There's a catch. Not a small one. The flat tax means you **forfeit every single deduction and credit** available under Korean tax law. No dependent deductions. No medical expense credits. No housing loan interest. Nothing.

Here's the break-even reality, calculated against actual 2025 tax brackets:

<ResponsiveTable>
| Gross Salary | Flat Tax (19%)[^1] | Progressive Rate | Better Choice |
|---|---|---|---|
| ₩50M | ₩9.92M | ₩3.57M | Progressive |
| ₩100M | ₩19.84M | ₩12.41M | Progressive |
| ₩160M | ₩31.75M | ₩29.95M | Progressive (barely) |
| ₩180M | ₩35.72M | ₩36.24M | Flat (saves ₩0.52M) |
| ₩200M | ₩39.69M | ₩43.01M | Flat (saves ₩3.32M) |
</ResponsiveTable>

[^1]: Flat tax column uses PwC-verified modeling that accounts for non-taxable allowances and the employment income deduction base. The effective rate on gross salary is slightly below the headline 20.9% (19% + 10% local surcharge) because non-taxable items reduce the base before the rate applies.

The crossover point sits somewhere between ₩160M and ₩180M depending on your deductions. Below that, progressive wins. Sometimes by a lot.

<Callout type="warning">
Under ₩120M gross? Don't even think about the flat tax. The progressive rate with standard deductions will save you millions of won.
</Callout>

A few things worth noting. The 20-year window (updated in 2023, used to be 5 years) sounds generous. It's not a gift — it's a lock-in. If your circumstances change, if you get married and have kids, if you buy a house, you've already given up those deductions. Think about whether your salary trajectory realistically ever hits ₩180M before making this election.

Also: the flat tax election must start **by December 31, 2026** to be valid under current rules. If you're considering it, talk to a 세무사 first. And read our breakdown of how Korea's tax treaties interact with this decision over at [Korea Double Tax Treaty Guide](/en/blog/tax/korea-double-tax-treaty-guide-2026) — treaty provisions can shift the math in unexpected ways.

## What Can Foreign Residents Actually Deduct?

The residency line matters here. If you spent **183 or more days in Korea** in the tax year, you're a tax resident. Resident status means you access essentially the same deduction structure as Korean nationals.

<ResponsiveTable>
| Deduction Type | Tax Resident (183+ days) | Non-Resident |
|---|---|---|
| Personal exemption (기본공제) | ₩1.5M per person | Not available |
| Dependent spouse/children | Available | Not available |
| National Health Insurance | Deductible | Not available |
| Credit card spending | 15-40% deduction | Not available |
| Medical expenses | 15% above 3% threshold | Not available |
| Education expenses | ₩3M limit per child | Not available |
| Housing savings (청약저축) | 40%, up to ₩3M/yr | Not available |
| Standard tax credit | ₩130,000 | Not available |
| Pension (NPS) contributions | Deductible | Not available |
</ResponsiveTable>

The foreign rent tax credit (월세공제) deserves its own deep-dive. See [Korea Rent Tax Credit for Foreigners](/en/blog/tax/korea-rent-tax-credit-foreigners-guide) for the full walkthrough, including what counts as a qualifying lease and how to claim it properly.

What you generally **can't deduct** regardless of residency status: overseas medical expenses paid at foreign hospitals, tuition paid to foreign schools, insurance premiums for foreign policies. These don't flow through the Korean tax system at all.

One thing many foreigners miss: dependent deductions aren't Korea-only. You can claim a non-resident foreign parent as a dependent if they meet the income and relationship criteria and you provide documentation. The paperwork is annoying. The deduction can be worth it.

## New for the 2025 Tax Year

Four changes in 2025 that are worth knowing before you file.

**Child credit increased (자녀세액공제).** Korea quietly made the numbers more interesting:

<ResponsiveTable>
| Number of Children (ages 8-20) | Old Credit | New Credit |
|---|---|---|
| 1st child | ₩150,000 | ₩250,000 |
| 2nd child | +₩150,000 | +₩300,000 |
| 3rd child and beyond | +₩300,000 | +₩400,000 |
</ResponsiveTable>

If you have two kids, that's ₩550,000 total — up from ₩300,000. Not life-changing, but real money.

**Gym and swimming pool deductions.** From July 1, 2025, membership fees for gyms (헬스장) and swimming pools (수영장) qualify for the 30% culture/sports expense deduction. Salary must be ₩70M or under. This one surprised people. Keep your receipts from the second half of the year.

**Newlywed credit (결혼세액공제).** ₩1,000,000 one-time credit for couples who married in 2025. Once per lifetime per person. Verify your specific eligibility with a 세무사, as the conditions for foreign couples may vary.

**Housing savings for foreign spouses.** Foreign spouses of non-homeowning household heads can now claim the 청약저축 (housing savings) deduction (조특법 §87): 40% of contributions, up to ₩3M per year, with a salary cap of ₩70M. This one flew under the radar. Confirm your eligibility with a 세무사, as the spouse-route criteria can be nuanced.

## How to File: Hometax for Foreigners

Hometax (홈택스, hometax.go.kr) is the NTS's online portal. You can do everything there: pull your 간소화서비스 data, check withholding records, submit corrections.

**What you need to log in:**
- Alien Registration Card (ARC) number
- Korean mobile phone number (for verification SMS)
- Kakao or PASS app, or a 공동인증서 (joint certificate) — the old 공인인증서 was abolished in 2021, good riddance

No Korean phone? You can sometimes use your bank's certificate or visit an NTS office in person. It's less smooth, but it works.

Once you're in, the **간소화서비스** auto-populates most of your data: credit card spending, health insurance, NPS contributions, medical bills paid through Korean institutions. Download the PDF or export the data directly.

What it **won't** have and you need to add manually:
- Rent receipts (월세) — the landlord has to report these, and many don't
- Glasses and contact lenses
- Foreign dependents (requires translated, apostilled documents)
- Cash transactions without receipts

The process is: download your 간소화서비스 data, review for gaps, collect missing receipts, submit everything to HR by their internal deadline, confirm your employer filed by March 10.

**No ARC?** If you're on a payroll without an ARC (rare, but it happens), your employer's HR/payroll team handles the filing mechanics. You still need to supply documentation for any deductions you want to claim.

NTS has an English help line: **1588-0560, press 7**. It's genuinely useful. The staff are patient.

## Special Situations

**Mid-year job change or departure.** If you left your job — or Korea — your employer does a 퇴직 시 정산 (separation settlement) at the time you leave. This covers tax reconciliation up to your departure date. If you had multiple jobs in the year, the last employer consolidates. You'll need a 근로소득원천징수영수증 (withholding receipt) from each prior employer.

**Foreign company salary (Class B / 을종 근로소득).** If you receive salary from a foreign company with no Korean withholding obligations, that's 을종 근로소득. Your employer is not withholding Korean tax. You are responsible for filing **종합소득세** (global income tax) on your own during May 1-31 each year. This is a separate process from 연말정산. Miss this and you're not just late — you're non-compliant.

**Freelancers and independent contractors.** 연말정산 doesn't apply to you. Your income is 사업소득 or 기타소득, and you file in May. Different forms, different rules. We cover the full flow in [Korea Freelancer Tax Filing Guide](/en/blog/tax/korea-freelancer-tax-filing-guide-2026).

**K-Tech Pass holders.** If you're on the K-Tech Pass (a special visa for tech talent in 8 designated fields), you may qualify for a 50% income tax reduction for up to 10 years. This interacts with 연말정산 in ways worth verifying with a tax professional before you assume the benefit applies automatically.

## Five Mistakes Foreigners Keep Making

**1. Trusting full automation.** 간소화서비스 is impressive. It pulls from dozens of institutions automatically. But it won't have your rent receipts, your glasses prescription, or documentation for foreign dependents. People who download and submit without reviewing leave real money behind.

**2. Assuming HR handles everything.** Your HR team files the consolidated return. They don't track down your receipts, verify your dependent eligibility, or flag that your spouse now qualifies for the housing savings deduction. That's your job.

**3. Forgetting the 10% local tax surcharge.** Income tax rates in Korea are always quoted before local tax. A 19% flat rate is actually 20.9%. A 24% bracket is actually 26.4%. This matters when you're running break-even calculations or comparing Korea's effective rate against your home country.

**4. Missing the flat-rate election window.** The 19% flat tax has an election deadline — you can't retroactively switch. If you've been in Korea a few years and never made this election, you're still on progressive. If you want to evaluate the flat tax option, do it before December 31, 2026.

**5. Not filing at all.** Korea has withholding, so technically you're paying tax throughout the year. But if you never submit your 연말정산 documents, you don't claim your deductions. Your employer will file based on what they have. You'll pay more than you owe, and you may not realize it.

## FAQ

### What is 연말정산?

연말정산 (year-end tax settlement) is the annual reconciliation process where Korea adjusts the income tax you paid throughout the year against what you actually owe based on your final income, deductions, and credits. It runs January-February for employment income, with a March 10 employer filing deadline.

### Can foreigners use Hometax?

Yes. Foreign residents with an ARC and a Korean mobile number can log into hometax.go.kr directly. The interface is primarily in Korean, but the NTS English helpline (1588-0560, press 7) can walk you through it. Some sections have English translations.

### What income tax rate do foreigners pay in Korea?

Tax residents pay the same progressive rates as Korean nationals: 6% on income up to ₩14M, scaling up to 45% on income over ₩1,000M, plus a 10% local income tax surcharge on all tiers. Foreign employees may alternatively elect a 19% flat rate (20.9% effective) for employment income, forfeiting all deductions. See the break-even table above before making that election.

### What is the filing deadline for 연말정산?

The employer's consolidated filing deadline is **March 10**. Your internal HR deadline is typically late January to mid-February — check with your company. If you miss HR's deadline, you can file corrections directly with the NTS. If you're on a foreign company payroll (Class B income), you file independently during May 1-31.

### Should I use the 19% flat tax?

Probably not, unless your salary exceeds ₩160-180M gross and you have minimal deductions. For most foreign workers in Korea, the progressive rate with standard deductions produces a lower effective tax bill. The flat tax is primarily relevant for very high earners or people who expect minimal deductible expenses over a long career in Korea. Run the numbers with a 세무사 before electing.
