## Gold Card: Who It's For

[11 professional fields](https://goldcard.nat.gov.tw/en/qualification/). The one most digital nomads care about: **Digital**, added in January 2025.

The others include Science & Technology, Economy, Education, Culture & Arts, Sports, Finance, Law, Architecture, and more. Each field has its own criteria: salary thresholds, publication records, award history. The Digital category opened the door for software engineers, product designers, and data people who previously didn't fit neatly into "Science & Technology."

Here's what the Gold Card actually changes about daily life in Taiwan:

**NHI from day one if employed or self-employed in Taiwan.** Taiwan's National Health Insurance (全民健康保險) covers hospital visits, prescriptions, dental, and more, for about NT$1,500/month. (Gold Card alone does not guarantee immediate enrollment; employment or 6 months of residence is required under the NHI Act.) DN Visa holders wait 6 months and buy private insurance in the meantime. That private plan costs $20–50 USD/month and covers less.

**Work for anyone.** Freelance for a Taipei startup. Teach a workshop. Consult for a local company. The DN Visa doesn't allow any of that. You're tied to your foreign employer.

**The tax math is real.** Salary above NT$3M per year gets a 50% exemption for your first 5 years. For someone earning NT$6M, NT$1.5M of the upper portion is tax-exempt, at marginal rates of 30–40%, that saves roughly NT$450,000–600,000 per year. Over five years, NT$2.25–3M stays in your pocket. (For a deeper breakdown, see our [183-day tax trap post](/en/blog/tips/183-day-tax-trap-digital-nomads).)

**APRC pathway.** The Gold Card builds toward Alien Permanent Resident Certificate (永久居留證). DN Visa doesn't. If Taiwan is your long-term base, this matters.

<Callout type="info">
The Gold Card application is fully online at [goldcard.nat.gov.tw](https://goldcard.nat.gov.tw/), but processing involves multiple government agencies. Budget 2–4 months. Don't wait until your current visa is about to expire.
</Callout>

## DN Visa: Who It's For

Different situation, different tool.

The DN Visa works if you earn your income from a company outside Taiwan and want a straightforward, fast visa. The bar is lower: $40,000 USD annual income if you're 30 or older, $20,000 if you're 20–29. Since January 2025, it's issued as a 6-month visa, extendable up to 2 years total.

You can't work for Taiwan companies on it. You can't freelance locally. But if your paycheck comes from overseas and you just want to live in Taipei for a year or two, it does the job.

Processing takes 2–4 weeks in most cases. Compare that to the Gold Card's 2–4 months. Speed matters when you're planning a move.

The 6-month NHI wait is the biggest pain point. You'll need private health insurance for those first months, and anyone who's had to use private insurance at a Taiwan hospital knows the coverage gap is noticeable.

## The Hybrid Strategy

Here's what a lot of people actually do. (My friend from that Taipei cafe? This was her move.)

**Step 1:** Apply for the DN Visa before you arrive. Fast approval, low friction. You're legal in Taiwan within weeks.

**Step 2:** Once you're settled (apartment signed, ARC in hand, bank account open), start the Gold Card application from inside Taiwan. You can apply while holding another valid visa.

**Step 3:** When the Gold Card comes through (2–4 months later), you switch over. NHI kicks in immediately. Local work rights unlock. The DN Visa served its purpose as a bridge.

This avoids the worst part of the Gold Card timeline: waiting months in your home country with no Taiwan visa while the application processes. Instead, you're already living your life in Taipei.

<Callout type="warning">
Verify with the [National Immigration Agency](https://www.immigration.gov.tw/) that your specific visa situation allows this transition. Rules on switching visa types while in-country can change. This describes a common approach based on published requirements, not a guaranteed process.
</Callout>

## FAQ

**Can I switch from DN Visa to Gold Card without leaving Taiwan?**
Based on published procedures, yes. Many people apply for the Gold Card while in Taiwan on another valid visa or ARC. The Gold Card application is online and doesn't require you to be in a specific country. Check current rules with NIA before starting.

**What if I don't meet any Gold Card field requirements?**
The DN Visa is your path. The income thresholds ($40K/$20K) are the main requirement, and the 6-month visa extendable up to 2 years since January 2025 makes it much more practical for longer stays. See our [full Taiwan DN Visa page](/en/taiwan/visa/dnv) for details.

**Is the Gold Card tax benefit worth it if I earn under NT$3M?**
The 50% salary exemption only applies above the NT$3M threshold. Below that, you're taxed at standard graduated rates either way. The Gold Card still gives you NHI from day one (if employed or self-employed in Taiwan) and local work rights, but the tax advantage specifically kicks in above NT$3M. Our [Taiwan visa comparison page](/en/taiwan/compare) has a full side-by-side breakdown.

## Pick Based on Your Situation

The Gold Card is the better long-term tool. NHI (if employed or self-employed in Taiwan), work flexibility, tax breaks, APRC pathway. If you can get it, get it.

The DN Visa is the better short-term tool. Fast, simple, good enough for 6–24 months of remote work from Taiwan.

And if you're not sure? Start with the DN Visa. Apply for the Gold Card once you're on the ground. You don't have to choose one forever.
