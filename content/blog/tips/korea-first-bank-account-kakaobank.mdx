Your landlord wants a ₩3 million deposit by Friday. You open a Korean bank account on your phone, move the money over and hit Transfer. The app will only let you send ₩1 million. You have the money. It's just in the wrong place.

If I were opening my first Korean account, I'd check my phone plan before picking a bank. A Korean number that gets texts from the delivery app can still fail a bank's identity check. Once you have a Korean residence ID and a line the bank can verify in your name, the choice gets easier: KakaoBank lets you take a selfie; Shinhan SOL Global lets you talk to someone on video.

Here for a month or two with just a passport? Neither app will open an account on that alone. Ask your landlord or host how they take the deposit before you book. If you do need a Korean account, call a branch first and ask what they'll accept for your residency status and what you need the account for.

## KakaoBank: Take a selfie

After you scan your ID, KakaoBank gives you a choice: take a selfie, make a video call, or confirm a ₩1 deposit in another Korean account. If this is your first account, use the selfie or the call.

KakaoBank is particular about the phone line. It has to be postpaid, in your name, and registered with an eligible Korean residence ID. A budget carrier is fine if it meets those conditions. A prepaid plan or a line registered with your passport won't pass. Already have a Korean number you want to keep? Ask your carrier if it can move that number to an eligible plan under your residence ID.

[KakaoBank's account-opening guide](https://blog.kakaobank.com/posts/foreign-account-opening) · [Document checklist](https://blog.kakaobank.com/posts/foreign-account-requirements)

## Shinhan SOL Global: Talk to someone on video

Shinhan's app for foreign residents accepts a Foreigner Registration Card, Permanent Resident Card or Domestic Residence Card. You'll also need a phone line in your name that passes the carrier check. If the check fails, compare the spelling, spaces and hyphens in your name on the carrier record with your ID.

Choose a video call with a Shinhan representative if you don't have another Korean account. The app comes in 16 languages; check which languages and hours are available for the call itself.

[Shinhan's English account-opening guide](https://solgit.shinhan.com/sc26090283.html) · [Shinhan's phone verification tips](https://solgit.shinhan.com/sc26090569.html)

## Can you send the deposit by Friday?

KakaoBank starts foreign-resident accounts with a ₩1 million daily transfer limit in the app. To send ₩3 million at once, ask the bank to lift the account restriction, then check your separate app transfer settings. At Shinhan, look up the limit on your new account. Ask what documents the bank needs to raise it and how long that takes.

If the money's due before the bank can review it, agree on another way to pay with the landlord before you move it.

[KakaoBank's guide to transfer limits](https://blog.kakaobank.com/posts/foreign-account-opening)

## What about Toss Bank and K Bank?

**Toss Bank.** An NFC passport can get you into the Toss app without a Korean number. Opening the bank account still takes an eligible Korean residence ID. The [English guide](https://toss.im/tossfeed/article/korealifehacks-7-en) doesn't spell out the verification step for someone who has never had a Korean account.

**K Bank.** It was still preparing remote account opening for foreign residents in [November reporting](https://www.etoday.co.kr/news/view/2650475). I couldn't confirm that it had launched, so I wouldn't count on it for Friday.
