## F-1-D: choose this route only if you can document the qualifying profile

The F-1-D expressly targets overseas business owners and people affiliated with overseas companies who can work remotely and have worked in the same industry for at least one year. It is available to eligible accompanying spouses and unmarried minor children. Its 2026 income table ranges from **₩52.416M to ₩104.832M**, based on the applicant's age, planned residence area, and family accompaniment—not a universal ₩88M threshold. The initial stay is one year; one-year extensions can reach a three-year maximum.

Read the full [F-1-D preparation guide](/en/blog/guides/korea-f1d-workation-visa-2026) before treating a regional reduction as available. You need evidence of a qualifying non-capital / population-decline-area stay, and the jurisdictional mission can request more documents.

The Ministry says employment and profit-making activities are restricted. That means F-1-D should not be presented as blanket authorization for Korean employment, Korean-market freelancing, or a Korean business. Ask immigration to assess your proposed arrangement.

## Short-term visitor status: entry is not a remote-work ruling

Short-term routes can be appropriate for an actual visit, but the entry period and whether a visa is needed depend on nationality and the purpose recorded in the application or at entry. The F-1-D guidance says people already in Korea on B-1, B-2, or C-3 may request an F-1-D change of status if eligible; it does **not** turn those short-term statuses into a general remote-work authorization.

We do not call remote work on visitor status a "grey zone" or recommend it as a workaround. Before travel, check the [Korea Visa Portal](https://www.visa.go.kr/openPage.do?LLANG=EN&MENU_ID=10105) and the responsible mission for your nationality, visa-exemption status, permitted stay, and planned activity.

## H-1 Working Holiday: a country-specific holiday program

The official Working Holiday Information Center describes H-1 as a one-year extended holiday with short-term work as secondary to the stay. It lists partner-country rules, quotas, age bands, and separate exceptions. Its general information says participants may work up to 25 hours a week, with a Canadian exception; employment periods and extensions can differ by agreement. It also says applicants normally apply and receive the visa in their country of nationality, and may not bring dependants under H-1.

For the standard route, the Center lists at least ₩3M funds for initial travel, return-travel proof or funds, travel plans, a criminal-record check, a medical certificate, and insurance of at least ₩40M. Those are general baseline conditions, not a promise that every mission applies the same checklist. Do not treat H-1 as an automatic alternative for a professional remote job or independent contracting; ask the mission how the agreement applies to your work.

## Quick scenarios

**You have a qualifying overseas employer or business, can prove a year in the industry, and plan a long stay.** Start with F-1-D. Pick the right 2026 income row before spending on documents.

**You plan a short visit and will not rely on work permission.** Check the visitor rule for your passport and trip purpose with the Visa Portal or mission. Do not infer remote-work permission from visa-free admission.

**You are a national of an H-1 partner country, meet its age and other rules, and your main purpose is a working holiday.** Check your country's H-1 notice and apply through the designated mission; do not generalize a different country's age, quota, or work rule.

**You are a freelancer, have Korean clients, expect Korean-source revenue, or need tax / insurance certainty.** These facts can change the legal analysis. Obtain individualized guidance from Korean immigration and, where relevant, a qualified Korean tax or insurance adviser before choosing a route.
