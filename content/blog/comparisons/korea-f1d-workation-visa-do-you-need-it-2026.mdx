## The Quick Answer

Korea's F-1-D Workation Visa exists for a reason — if you earn steady remote income and plan to stay longer than 90 days, it solves real problems. But it comes with a high income bar and zero path to residency.

## F-1-D vs Just Staying on B-2 Tourist Visa

The real decision tree isn't just about having the F-1-D option — it's whether it's worth the paperwork and costs compared to tourist visas.

The single biggest difference: **F-1-D gives you an Alien Registration Card (ARC)**, which unlocks Korean infrastructure. Without an ARC on B-2, you'll spend months begging landlords for apartments and fighting with telecom companies.

## Three Scenarios: Which Visa Is For You?

<Callout type="info">
**Scenario 1: "I want 2–3 months in Seoul, then move on"**

Use B-2 tourist visa. You don't need F-1-D. No income requirement, visa-free entry (if eligible), zero paperwork. Coworking spaces, shared housing, and digital nomad communities won't ask for an ARC. Single trip, minimal risk.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip">
**Scenario 2: "I earn $70K+ annually and want to stay a year (or more)"**

Apply for F-1-D. The income bar ($66K) is real, but if you clear it, the ARC is game-changing. You can open bank accounts, sign apartment leases, buy a Korean phone plan, and stop explaining yourself to landlords. The ₩100K–₩300K application fee + insurance cost (₩100M coverage) is worth it for infrastructure access.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning">
**Scenario 3: "I earn ~$50K and want to stay 6 months"**

You don't qualify for F-1-D (income too low). Stick with B-2 tourist (90 days) + one extension (up to 180 days). You'll live in the gray zone of remote work, but that's the trade-off for lower income. Some remote workers do this successfully, but expect friction with landlords and financial institutions.
</Callout>

## Important Limitations

The F-1-D visa does NOT:

- Give you a clear path to permanent residency or long-term settlement
- Allow you to work for Korean employers or take on clients in Korea without additional permits
- Lead to F-2 or F-5 (residency) status after your 2 years expire
- Cover medical costs beyond the ₩100M insurance requirement (which is basic catastrophic coverage)

If you're thinking "I'll get F-1-D now and transition to F-5 later," that's not how it works. F-1-D is purely for temporary remote work. Plan accordingly.
