## Korea: The 6-Month Wait

[Korea’s NHIS guidance](https://www.nhis.or.kr/english/wbheaa02900m01.do) gives six months as the general mandatory enrollment threshold for eligible foreign regional subscribers and lists F-1 among the qualifying status categories. It does not provide a separate F-1-D determination in that guidance.

**For F-1-D holders (updated Nov 2026)**: Ask NHIS to confirm your eligibility, enrollment date and premium for your exact status before budgeting for public coverage. Do not treat six months as automatic enrollment or as permission to cancel your private policy.

**What it costs**: If enrolled, your premium depends on your subscriber category and NHIS assessment. Ask NHIS for the amount rather than assuming a quoted monthly figure applies to you. Workplace insurance has different rules and is not implied by holding a visa.

**What it covers**: Identical to Korean citizens. Outpatient visits, hospitalization, prescriptions, preventive care, dental (basic treatments — not implants or cosmetic). Walk-in clinics (의원) are fast, cheap, and everywhere. A typical visit with prescription costs ₩30,000–50,000 out of pocket after NHIS.

There's an annual copay ceiling tied to income. Spend beyond it and NHIS reimburses the excess.

**Private insurance (updated Nov 2026)**: For the visa application, [Korea Immigration](https://www.immigration.go.kr/sites/immigration/file/ebook/gongzone/2026/2026_gongzone_vol77.pdf) requires personal medical insurance of at least **₩100 million**. The [immigration insurance guidance](https://immigration.go.kr/bbs/immigration/47/464289/download.do) specifies treatment and repatriation during the stay. Check the certificate and duration required by the mission handling your application. Examples of providers to research:

- [SafetyWing Nomad Insurance](https://safetywing.com/) — ~$45–69/month depending on age
- [Allianz Travel Insurance](https://www.allianztravel.com/) — comprehensive plans from ~$60/month
- [World Nomads](https://www.worldnomads.com/) — good for shorter coverage windows

Do not cancel the private plan based only on time spent in Korea. Confirm coverage and continuing visa conditions with NHIS and the immigration authority first.

For the full financial picture in Korea: [Korea digital nomad guide](/en/blog/guides/korea-ultimate-digital-nomad-guide) and [leaving Korea money checklist](/en/blog/tax/leaving-korea-money-checklist-2026) (NHIS cancellation is on that list — skip it and bills keep coming even after you leave).

## Japan: You're On Your Own

Japan's healthcare system is excellent. The problem is that digital nomad visa holders cannot use it.

**Why the visa doesn’t give you NHI**: Japan’s [Immigration Services Agency](https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/applications/status/designatedactivities10_00001?hl=en) states that this status does not issue a residence card. Plan on private cover for a stay under this status.

The immigration designation does not determine your tax treatment.

**What you need instead**: Japan requires insurance against death, injury and illness for the full stay, with [at least **¥10 million** in medical treatment benefits](https://www.mofa.go.jp/ca/fna/pagewe_000001_00046.html) for injury or illness (updated Nov 2026). Check the actual certificate before buying. Examples of providers to research:

- [SafetyWing Nomad Insurance](https://safetywing.com/) — ¥15,000–20,000/month ($100–135)
- [World Nomads](https://www.worldnomads.com/) — ¥20,000–30,000/month ($135–200) for higher coverage
- [Allianz Global](https://www.allianztravel.com/) — comprehensive plans from ~¥18,000/month

**What healthcare costs without NHI**:

The ambulance is free regardless of insurance status (call 119). Hospitals cannot refuse emergency patients. But they typically want upfront payment from uninsured foreigners, so carry your insurance card and a credit card everywhere.

**For work visa holders**: Ask your employer or local municipality which public plan applies; some workers are covered by employee health insurance rather than municipal NHI. Premiums and copays depend on the plan and your circumstances.

**Coming in 2027**: Japan announced that foreign residents with unpaid NHI premiums or pension contributions will in principle be ineligible for visa renewals (原則不許可). This affects work visa holders on NHI, not DN visa holders (who aren't on NHI to begin with).

## Taiwan: Two Tiers

Taiwan's NHI is one of the best public healthcare systems in Asia. Whether you get access depends entirely on which visa you hold.

### Gold Card: NHI (With Conditions)

[Gold Card](/en/taiwan/visa/gold-card) holders’ enrollment timing depends on the [qualifying activity and residence requirements](https://goldcard.nat.gov.tw/en/tags/national-health-insurance/) (updated Nov 2026):

- **Qualifying employment in Taiwan**: Enrollment starts on the employment date, with your employer arranging it.
- **Qualifying local employer or self-employed business owner**: Enrollment can start from the date you qualify, through your business’s NHI insured unit. Overseas freelance work by itself does not establish that category.
- **Neither category**: Generally, six months of qualifying residence are required. The waiting period for eligible dependents also depends on the principal holder’s qualifying status and the dependents’ residence documents.

**Premium rates** ([official calculation](https://goldcard.nat.gov.tw/en/faq/how-are-premiums-calculated-and-collected-for-foreign-professionals-and-their-dependents/)): Your contribution depends on insured category, salary or another applicable contribution base:

**What NHI covers**: Outpatient, inpatient, dental (including 2 free cleanings per year), prescriptions, traditional Chinese medicine (acupuncture, herbal medicine), rehabilitation, mental health (outpatient psychiatry), emergency care.

Prescription drug copay maxes out at NT$300 per visit. A clinic visit runs NT$50 (~$2). This is why Gold Card holders with local employment have such a significant advantage over every other nomad visa in East Asia.

### Digital Nomad Visitor Visa: Plan on Private Insurance

The [Digital Nomad Visa](/en/taiwan/visa/dnv) is a separate **visitor visa**, not the Gold Card or a residence permit. Its [extensions](https://foreigntalentact.ndc.gov.tw/foreigntalentact_en/Content_List.aspx?n=829E8FB4B4C21175) can bring the total stay to **two years**; time on this visitor visa does not itself satisfy [NHI’s residence documentation requirement](https://law.moj.gov.tw/ENG/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=L0060001) (updated Nov 2026).

[BOCA](https://www.boca.gov.tw/cp-158-7718-c0382-2.html) requires proof of health and full hospitalization cover for the entire intended stay. Examples of providers to research:

- [SafetyWing](https://safetywing.com/) — ~$50–61/month for ages 18–39
- [Cigna Global](https://www.cigna.com/global/) — comprehensive international plans
- [AXA Global](https://www.axa.com/) — broad Asia-Pacific coverage

Without NHI, a clinic visit runs NT$500–1,500 ($16–47). That's 10x the NHI copay. Dental, traditional Chinese medicine, and mental health are all full price.

The NHI gap is one of the strongest arguments for Gold Card over DNV — for applicants whose credentials fall within one of [Taiwan's 12 professional fields](https://goldcard.nat.gov.tw/en/qualification/), the healthcare savings alone may justify the application effort. More on this: [Gold Card vs DN Visa](/en/blog/tips/taiwan-gold-card-vs-dn-visa-2026).

**Korea visa rules (updated Nov 2026)**: The [Ministry of Justice](https://www.immigration.go.kr/bbs/immigration/214/608294/artclView.do) raised the F-1-D maximum stay to **three years** with extensions and replaced the flat income threshold with **1–2 times the preceding year’s per-capita GNI**, depending on age, place of residence and applicable family conditions. This is the visa test, not a public insurance test.

**Japan for short stays**: Compare private policies that meet the visa requirements. Do not budget for a blanket Japanese tax exemption: the [National Tax Agency](https://www.nta.go.jp/english/taxes/individual/12006.htm) notes that remuneration for work performed in Japan can be Japan-source. Treaty relief and individual facts can affect the result.

**Taiwan for qualifying residents**: If you qualify for the Gold Card, confirm when your employment or business will enroll you. Otherwise budget for private cover until you meet the applicable residence requirement.

**Taiwan on the visitor route**: Budget for private cover for the full intended stay, including any period you plan to extend.

## Private Insurance: What to Look For

Every excluded nomad needs private coverage. Here's what matters for each visa:

**Coverage minimums (visa requirements)**:
- Japan DN visa: ¥10,000,000 (~$67,000) — non-negotiable
- Korea F-1-D: ₩100 million in medical/repatriation coverage; confirm evidence and duration with your consulate
- Taiwan DNV: health + hospitalization coverage — amount not specified but enforced

**What to check in your policy**:
- Outpatient coverage (not just hospitalization — many budget plans skip this)
- Emergency medical evacuation
- Pre-existing condition exclusions and waiting periods
- Claims process in-country: cashless network vs. reimbursement
- Coverage territory (some plans exclude specific countries or charge extra for Japan)

## FAQ

- **Can I use my home country's insurance in East Asia?**

Depends entirely on your plan. Most domestic health insurance doesn't cover overseas care, or only covers emergencies with reimbursement (not direct billing). Japan's DN visa specifically requires insurance valid in Japan with ¥10M coverage — most home plans don't meet this. Check your policy's "territorial scope" clause before assuming you're covered.

- **What if I need emergency surgery and I'm uninsured?**

All three countries provide emergency care regardless of insurance status. Japan and Korea hospitals may require upfront payment or a credit card guarantee from uninsured patients. Taiwan hospitals typically treat first and bill after. In all cases, costs without insurance run 3–10x higher. Keep your insurance documentation accessible at all times — phone photo of the policy card at minimum.

- **I'm on Taiwan's DNV but want NHI. Can I switch to Gold Card?**

If you meet a Gold Card [professional qualification](https://goldcard.nat.gov.tw/en/qualification/), verify your current visa and in-country options with the [Gold Card Office](https://goldcard.nat.gov.tw/en/application/). Do not count on switching before your visitor stay expires. NHI access then depends on your qualifying activity or residence.

- **Does Korea's NHIS cover dependents?**

Do not assume enrollment or dependent coverage from the visa alone. Ask [NHIS](https://www.nhis.or.kr/english/wbheaa02900m01.do) which relationship documents, residence requirements and enrollment category apply.
