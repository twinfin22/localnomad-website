## Duration: Who Lets You Stay

Korea’s [F-1-D visa](/en/korea/visa/f-1-d) starts with one year. Apply for one-year extensions, subject to approval, up to a maximum of 3 years. (updated Nov 2026) [Korean Ministry of Justice](https://www.immigration.go.kr/bbs/immigration/214/608294/artclView.do)

Japan's digital nomad visa is 6 months. Hard stop. You cannot renew it inside Japan. You must leave, then reapply from abroad. Back-to-back stays are not possible by design. Japan built the visa for travelers, not settlers.

Taiwan's Gold Card runs 1 to 3 years, and you can renew it indefinitely as long as your qualification holds. Uncertain about long-term Asia plans? Taiwan is the only one of the three that keeps that door open.

For a stay beyond a year, compare Korea’s annual F-1-D extensions with Taiwan’s Gold Card renewal rules. Japan’s digital nomad status is not extendable.

## Income: What You Need to Earn

**Korea: annual income requirements vary**

Income thresholds use the previous year’s per-capita gross national income (GNI). For applicants aged 18–34, the threshold is 1.5× GNI in the capital region and 1× in qualifying regional areas. For ages 35+ or applicants bringing family, it is 2× and 1.5×, respectively. Verify the figure and address eligibility with the mission handling your application. (updated Nov 2026) [Korean Ministry of Justice](https://www.immigration.go.kr/bbs/immigration/214/608294/artclView.do)

Worked example (updated Nov 2026): the Singapore embassy’s 2026 table uses ₩52,416,000 as the GNI base, giving thresholds of ₩52,416,000 (1×), ₩78,624,000 (1.5×) or ₩104,832,000 (2×). This is the Singapore mission’s published basis, not a universal fixed amount. [Korean Embassy in Singapore](https://overseas.mofa.go.kr/sg-en/brd/m_2444/view.do?seq=761493)

If you claim a regional reduction, check the address and proof of stay with the mission handling your application.

For the full application breakdown, see our [F-1-D guide](/en/blog/guides/korea-f1d-workation-visa-2026).

**Japan: ¥10 million (~$68K/year)**

Japan requires [proof of annual income of at least ¥10 million](https://www.mofa.go.jp/ca/fna/pagewe_000001_00046.html). Check its separate document requirements with the mission handling your application.

**Taiwan Employment Gold Card: qualification by field**

Taiwan evaluates candidates by qualification field rather than a flat income bar. You must fit one of [12 professional fields](https://goldcard.nat.gov.tw/en/qualification/) including Digital, Science & Technology, Finance, Economy, Culture and Arts, Architecture, and more, as defined by Taiwan's National Development Council. Most fields do have a salary route (NTD 160K+/month), but portfolio-based applications are possible in several categories. Proof includes: GitHub repos, published work, certifications, degrees, client letters.

[goldcard.nat.gov.tw/en/qualification](https://goldcard.nat.gov.tw/en/qualification/)

Taiwan also has a digital nomad visitor visa, separate from the Gold Card in this table. It is open to nationals of visa-exempt countries who meet the visitor route’s requirements. The Gold Card’s residency benefits do not describe that visitor route. (updated Nov 2026) [Taiwan Bureau of Consular Affairs](https://www.boca.gov.tw/cp-158-7718-c0382-2.html)

## Tax Rules

This is where most nomads get surprised.

**Japan: check income source and tax-treaty rules**

Japan taxes nonresidents on Japanese-source income. Pay for work performed in Japan may be Japanese-source even when the employer or client is overseas.

Do not assume that a digital nomad visa or a foreign payer means no Japanese tax.

See [Japan’s National Tax Agency](https://www.nta.go.jp/english/taxes/individual/12006.htm) for the income-source and tax-treaty rules.

**Korea: 183-day worldwide tax**

Spend 183+ days in Korea in a calendar year and you're a tax resident. That means worldwide income is subject to Korean rates: 6% at the bottom bracket up to 45% at the top, plus local income tax (10% of national PIT). Remote clients in the US? Taxed. Rental income elsewhere? Taxed. Foreign dividends? Taxed.

There are foreign tax credits, so you won't always pay twice. But you'll file in two countries and manage the paperwork. Korea has tax treaties with [~97 countries](https://taxlaw.nts.go.kr/st/USESTC001M.do). Check whether yours is on the list — [NTS English guidance](https://www.nts.go.kr/english/cm/cntnts/cntntsView.do?mi=20251&cntntsId=8810) has the details. The 183-day mechanics explained in detail: [our tax trap post](/en/blog/tax/183-day-tax-trap-digital-nomads).

**Taiwan: Tiered, with a high-earner kicker**

Under 183 days in Taiwan: flat 18% on Taiwan-source income only. Over 183 days: graduated 5–40% on worldwide income, same structure as Korea.

Gold Card holders get one extra: a 50% tax exemption on salary above NT$3 million (~$98K) for the first five years of residence. Earn NT$6M, pay tax on NT$3M. That's a real number for senior engineers or finance professionals.

Taiwan has tax treaties with [35 jurisdictions](https://www.mof.gov.tw/eng/singlehtml/264?cntId=82780), fewer than Korea or Japan, so check your home country first. Full breakdown in the [Gold Card tax FAQ](https://goldcard.nat.gov.tw/en/faq/what-is-the-legal-bases-of-enacting-tax-benefits-for-foreign-specialist-professionals-and-what-are-the-benefits/).

Simplest tax situation: Japan. Best for high earners committing 3–5 years: Taiwan. Most paperwork: Korea.

## Housing and Daily Life

Visa status shapes what housing is actually available to you on the ground.

**Korea: ARC opens the standard market**

F-1-D holders get a full 외국인등록증 (ARC, Alien Registration Card). Once registered, you can sign a standard lease with most landlords and open a bank account at Woori or Hana in about 30 minutes.

Two rental formats dominate:

- **월세 (wolse)**: Monthly rent. Expect a 보증금 (deposit) of ₩5–15M locked for the lease term, plus monthly rent of ₩900K–1.5M for a central one-bedroom (2026 prices). The deposit is refundable at move-out but tied up for 1–2 years.
- **전세 (jeonse)**: A lump-sum deposit of ₩500M+ in exchange for zero monthly rent. Not practical for most digital nomads.

Wolse is the realistic option. Budget for the deposit lock-up.

[ALLO Korea Housing Guide 2026](https://allo-korea.com/en/housing-in-korea-guide-2026/) | [Seoul vs Tokyo cost comparison](/en/blog/comparisons/seoul-vs-tokyo-cost-of-living-2026)

**Japan: No ARC, guarantor market**

Japan's DN visa does not come with an ARC or residential registration. Landlords typically require a guarantor (保証人, a Japanese citizen or permanent resident who co-signs and accepts liability). No legal mandate, but it's standard market practice.

[University of Tokyo housing guidance](https://www.u-tokyo.ac.jp/adm/housing-office/en/useful_tips/1-06.html) | [GTN (guarantor service)](https://www.gtn.co.jp/magazine/en_us/article205/)

Workaround: use a guarantor service (¥5K–10K/month), or book furnished apartments in serviced housing where management handles the guarantee.

Without residential registration, certain services become unavailable: library cards, some healthcare registrations, local government tools. You can live there. But you won't be registered there.

[Japan housing deep-dive: digital nomads 2026](/en/blog/guides/japan-housing-digital-nomads-2026)

**Taiwan: ARC, standard market, full banking**

Gold Card comes with an ARC. Landlords don't require guarantors. CTBC, Cathay United, and Fubon all open accounts for ARC holders. Apple Pay, Google Pay, and mobile payments work across the board.

[goldcard.nat.gov.tw](https://goldcard.nat.gov.tw/en/)

Korea and Taiwan are both practical for housing and daily banking. Japan requires workarounds at almost every step.

## Which Visa Fits Which Nomad

**Staying one to three years, subject to renewal**
Consider Korea F-1-D. The initial year can be extended in one-year increments up to 3 years, subject to approval. Foreign residents can register for a residence card.

**A six-month trial stay**
Consider Japan’s digital nomad visa if you meet its income requirement and want a short stay. Check your tax position separately.

**High earner, 3–5 year plan, PR on the table**
Taiwan Gold Card. The 50% salary exemption above NT$3M is real money for senior earners. The PR path requires 3 years of continuous residence (183+ days/yr) — still faster than the 5+ years on most standard paths.

**Building a base with family**
Taiwan again. Gold Card spouses can apply to the Ministry of Labor (MOL) for an open work permit — no employer sponsorship needed, but it's not automatic. Korea allows dependents on F-1-D but spouses can't work freely. Japan's DN Visa explicitly allows spouse and children to be included in the same application, receiving the same 6-month stay.

## FAQ

- **Can I switch between these visas if my plans change?**

Yes, but each country treats it differently. In Korea, switching from F-1-D to another status (like [E-7](/en/korea/visa/e-7) or [F-2](/en/korea/visa/f-2)) requires a new application at immigration. It's not automatic. In Japan, because the DN visa must be reapplied from abroad anyway, switching to a different visa category means starting a fresh application. In Taiwan, Gold Card holders can transition to resident status, entrepreneur visas, or other categories if their situation changes. Check current requirements at each country's immigration office before planning a switch.

- **Which country is cheapest to live in day-to-day?**

Korea is generally the lowest cost of the three for central city living. Seoul's wolse rents run lower than Tokyo for comparable apartments, groceries are reasonable, and public transit is cheap. Taiwan sits in the middle: Taipei is cheaper than Tokyo, slightly more than Seoul, with lower food costs than both. Japan is the most expensive, particularly for housing in central Tokyo and dining out. For city-by-city numbers, see our [Seoul vs Tokyo cost breakdown](/en/blog/comparisons/seoul-vs-tokyo-cost-of-living-2026).
