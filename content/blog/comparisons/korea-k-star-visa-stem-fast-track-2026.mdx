## The Quick Answer

Korea launched K-STAR (Korea Scholarship for Talented Research) in 2023 to hold onto STEM talent. If you graduated from one of 32 participating universities with a master's or PhD in AI, biotechnology, or semiconductors, you can skip the 6-year standard wait for permanent residency and do it in 3 years instead.

The biggest difference: **K-STAR removes two bottlenecks**. First, you get visa status the day you graduate—no job hunt required. Second, you skip the point system that rejects thousands of F-2-7 applicants annually.

## Who Qualifies for K-STAR

Your degree must check all three boxes:

<Callout type="info">
**Eligibility Requirements:**

1. **Master's or PhD in STEM** — From one of the 32 approved K-STAR universities in Korea
2. **University president recommendation** — Your institution must nominate you; you cannot apply directly
3. **STEM field** — AI, machine learning, biotechnology, robotics, data science, semiconductor engineering, advanced materials science, quantum computing, or related disciplines

**Confirmed universities (partial list):** KAIST, GIST, UNIST, DGIST, UST, Seoul National University. The full list of 32 is still being published; contact your university's international student office to confirm eligibility.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning">
**Important Unknowns (as of March 2026):**

- Full list of 32 approved universities not yet fully public (only ~6 confirmed)
- Family sponsorship details and timing still being finalized by immigration ministry
- Performance review criteria for the 3-year F-5 assessment not yet detailed
- Bachelor's degree holders eligibility unclear (sources currently say master's/PhD only)
- Whether you must remain in Korea during the 3-year period to qualify

Contact immigration.go.kr or korea.net for updates.
</Callout>

## Why Korea Is Doing This

Korea faces a talent drain. Top graduates choose US PhD programs or move to Europe. Meanwhile, Korea's aging population and global competition in semiconductors and AI means it can't afford to lose STEM talent. K-STAR is the shortcut: offer residents faster PR, and you keep the people building Korea's tech future.

## Important Notes

K-STAR is young and still expanding. The program announced in 2023 and approved ~300 graduates through 2026, but many details remain fluid.
