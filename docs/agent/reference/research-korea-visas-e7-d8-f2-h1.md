## E-7 — Specific Activities (Professional Employment)

### Overview
- Visa for foreign nationals with specialized knowledge/skills employed by Korean organizations
- 4 sub-categories: E-7-1 (Professional, 67 occupations), E-7-2 (Semi-professional, 10), E-7-3 (Skilled Labor, 10), E-7-4 (Point-based, 3)
- **Employer sponsorship required** — cannot self-apply

### Eligibility
- Master's degree or higher (no work experience needed), OR
- Bachelor's degree + 1 year related work experience, OR
- 5+ years career experience (no degree needed)
- Special: 1 year at World Top 500 company, QS 500/THE 200 university graduates, Korean university graduates exempt from career requirement
- As of April 2025: 18-24 year olds with long-term Korea stay can get E-7 without degree

### Employer Requirements
- Must have 5+ national employees
- Foreigners limited to 20% of national employees
- Must prove necessity of hiring foreigner (invitation letter, utilization plan)
- Employment recommendation letter from relevant government agency

### Salary Requirements (2026, effective Feb 1 – Dec 31)
- E-7-1 (Professional): ≥ ₩31,120,000/year
- E-7-2 (Semi-professional): ≥ ₩25,890,000/year

### Duration
- Initial: up to 3 years (usually 1 year)
- Extension: possible, up to 5 years in special cases

### Fees
- Application: ~₩60,000 (extension) / ~USD 60-100 (embassy, varies by nationality)
- Processing: 2-4 weeks (CCVI stage) + 5-10 business days (embassy stamping)

### Required Documents
1. Passport
2. Integrated application form
3. Alien Registration Card (if in Korea)
4. Photo (within 6 months)
5. Employment contract
6. Statement of employment
7. Employment recommendation letter (if required)
8. Company documents (business license, registration certificate)
9. Documents proving foreigner's qualification (degree, career certificates)
10. Foreigner utilization plan / invitation letter
- Foreign documents: Korean/English translation + apostille/consular notarization

### Transition Paths
- FROM: D-10 (job seeking), D-2 (student), F-1-D (digital nomad)
- TO: F-2-7 (points-based resident, after meeting score), F-5 (permanent resident)

## D-8 — Corporate Investment

### Overview
- For foreign nationals managing a company they've invested in or established in Korea
- Sub-categories: D-8-1 (foreign-invested corp), D-8-2 (tech transfer), D-8-4 (startup)
- Most common: D-8-1

### Eligibility
- Minimum investment: **₩100,000,000** (~$75,000 USD)
- Must own ≥10% of voting shares OR own shares + executive dispatch contract
- Must actively manage the business (passive investment doesn't qualify)
- Investment funds must be legitimate and personally generated

### Source of Funds
- Remitter must be investor, spouse, or minor child
- If >₩300M: parents of investor/spouse also allowed
- Corporate investor: remittance under corporation name
- Must provide: business registration, sales records, financial statements, bank transaction history

### Duration
- Initial: 1 year
- Extension: based on business performance, up to 5 years

### Fees
- Application: ~₩60,000 (extension)
- Processing: 4-8 weeks (embassy) / 2-4 weeks (change of status in Korea)
- Company registration: 2-3 weeks (up to 4 weeks with bank account)

### Required Documents
1. Passport
2. Application form
3. Company registration documents
4. FDI (Foreign Direct Investment) registration
5. Bank receipts / Foreign Currency Purchase Certificate (외국환매입증명서)
6. Tax records
7. Business plan
8. Criminal record
9. Health insurance
10. Articles of incorporation (notarized)
11. Securities subscription deposit certificate

### Transition Paths
- FROM: F-1-D (digital nomad), E-7 (professional), D-10 (job seeking)
- TO: F-2 (resident), F-5 (permanent resident — requires ₩500M+ investment or $500K+ for 3+ years)

### Key Warnings
- Must report investment in advance before capital remittance (or 50% tax penalty)
- If business closes: notify immigration within 30 days
- Family: spouse + children under 20 can get F-3 dependent visas

## F-2 — Resident (F-2-7 Points-Based)

### Overview
- Long-term residence visa for talented professionals
- Points-based system: need ≥80 points out of 170 total
- Key benefit: job flexibility (can change jobs, start business), path to F-5 permanent residency

### Eligibility
- Must hold qualifying visa (E-1~E-7, D-5~D-9) for 3+ consecutive years, OR
- Annual salary ≥₩40M (can apply immediately regardless of stay length), OR
- Korean master's/doctoral graduate employed within 5 years of degree

### Points System (Total: 170, need ≥80)

**Age (max 25):**
| Age | Points |
|-----|--------|
| 18-24 | 23 |
| 25-29 | 25 |
| 30-34 | 23 |
| 35-39 | 20 |
| 40-44 | 12 |
| 45-50 | 8 |
| 51+ | 3 |

**Education (max 25):**
| Degree | Science/2+ Majors | Other |
|--------|-------------------|-------|
| Ph.D. | 25 | 20 |
| Master's | 20 | 17 |
| Bachelor's | 17 | 15 |
| Associate | 15 (eng) | 10 |

**Korean Language — TOPIK/KIIP (max 20):**
| Level | Points |
|-------|--------|
| Level 1 | 3 |
| Level 2 | 5 |
| Level 3 | 10 |
| Level 4 | 15 |
| Level 5-6 | 20 |

**Annual Income (max 60):**
| Income (KRW) | Points |
|--------------|--------|
| ≥₩100M | 60 |
| ₩90-100M | 58 |
| ₩80-90M | 56 |
| ₩70-80M | 53 |
| ₩60-70M | 50 |
| ₩50-60M | 45 |
| ₩40-50M | 40 |
| ₩30-40M | 30 |
| Min wage-₩30M | 10 |

**Bonus Points:**
- KIIP Level 5 completion: +10
- Korean university Bachelor: +5
- Korean university Master/PhD: +10
- QS Top 500 degree: +15 to +30
- Community service ≥3 years: +7
- Korean War participant country: +20
- Government recommendation: +20

### Stay Duration by Score
| Score | Duration |
|-------|----------|
| ≥130 or income ≥50pts | 5 years |
| 120-129 or income ≥45pts | 3 years |
| 110-119 or income ≥40pts | 2 years |
| 80-109 or income ≥30pts | 1 year |

### Fees
- First-time acquisition: ~₩85,000
- Extension: ₩60,000

### Required Documents
1. Application form
2. Passport + Alien Registration Card
3. Photo
4. Proof of residency (housing contract)
5. Criminal background check (home country, apostilled, if first-time or left Korea 6+ months)
6. Employment contract / business license
7. Income certificate (근로소득증명서)
8. Degree certificates
9. TOPIK/KIIP certificates
10. Tuberculosis screening certificate
11. Family relationship documents (if applicable)

### Key Benefits
- Can change jobs freely
- Can start own business
- Spouse/children can get F-2-71 visa (and work)
- Path to F-5 permanent residency after 3 years
- Korean language requirement temporarily suspended until Dec 31, 2026 (if other requirements met)

- https://immikorea.com/en/f-2-7-visa-point-based-visa-score-table/
- https://kowork.kr/en/visa/F-2-7
- https://koreaprcalculator.com/
- https://visaofkorea.com/en/f-2-7-point-based-residence-visa-2/

## H-1 — Working Holiday

### Overview
- For young people (18-30, some countries 18-35) from 25+ agreement countries
- Stay and work in Korea for up to 1-2 years while traveling
- One-time only (exceptions: US, Ireland, Sweden can reapply)

### Eligible Countries (25+)
Australia, Canada, New Zealand, Japan, United States, France, Germany, Ireland, Sweden, Denmark, Hong Kong, Taiwan, Czech Republic, Italy, United Kingdom, Austria, Hungary, Israel, Netherlands, Portugal, Belgium, Chile, Poland, Spain, Argentina

### Age Limit
- General: 18-30 (inclusive)
- UK & Canada: 18-35

### Requirements
- Valid passport (6+ months remaining)
- No serious criminal record
- Health insurance valid throughout stay (minimum coverage: ₩40,000,000)
- No dependents allowed
- Proof of funds: minimum ₩3,000,000 + return ticket
- Must apply from home country
- Must not have held H-1 before (except US, Ireland, Sweden)
- US applicants: must be post-secondary student or recent graduate (within 1 year)

### Duration (varies by country)
- Japan: single entry, 1 year
- US: multiple entry, 18 months
- Canada, UK: multiple entry, 2 years
- Extensions: US (up to 6 months additional), UK (up to 1 year additional)

### Work Conditions
- Max 25 hours/week
- Allowed: hospitality, retail, translation, internships
- NOT allowed: English teaching, skilled professions, professional entertainment

### Required Documents
1. Valid passport (6+ months)
2. Passport-size photo (2" x 2")
3. Health insurance certificate (coverage ≥₩40M)
4. Bank statements (last 3 months, showing ≥₩3M)
5. Completed visa application form
6. Return flight ticket or proof of funds
- Additional documents may vary by embassy/consulate

### Fees
- Varies by nationality and consulate
- Generally ~USD 50-100

### After Arrival
- Must register for ARC within 90 days

### Transition Paths
- TO: D-4 (language study), D-10 (job seeking), E-7 (if find employment)

## Community Tips (curated from blogs, expat sites, forums)

> Note: Reddit search did not return direct results. Tips below are from expat blogs (britadventures, 10mag, ctrlaltkorea, gogohanguk), immigration advisory sites (KOWORK, IMMIKOREA, bangduty, allvisakorea), and community guides (Medium, Shuttle Delivery Partners, pvtistes.net).

### E-7 Community Tips

1. **Job code selection is critical**
   - "Choosing the wrong occupational code is one of the biggest reasons for E-7 rejections. Work with your employer or an immigration specialist to match the exact code."
   - Source: allvisakorea.com, KOWORK
   - Verified: true

2. **The employment reason statement makes or breaks your application**
   - "Your company must explain why they need a foreigner instead of a Korean national. Generic reasons get rejected. Be specific about your unique skills."
   - Source: bangduty.com, IMMIKOREA
   - Verified: true

3. **Korean university graduates have significant advantages**
   - "If you graduated from a Korean university, you're exempt from career requirements regardless of your major. This is the easiest path to E-7."
   - Source: dev-korea.com, KOWORK
   - Verified: true

4. **Pangyo/Bundang startups are more English-friendly**
   - "Korea's Silicon Valley area (Pangyo, Bundang) has many startups comfortable with English-speaking teams. AI, blockchain, cybersecurity have highest demand."
   - Source: dev-korea.com
   - Verified: true

5. **Start the process before your current visa expires**
   - "The review usually takes 1.5 to 2 months. Even if your visa expires during review, the expiration date is extended. But submit at least 30 days before expiry to avoid fines."
   - Source: KOWORK, kimchilandguide.com
   - Verified: true

### D-8 Community Tips

1. **Register FDI BEFORE transferring money**
   - "You must report your foreign direct investment in advance before capital remittance. Skip this step and face a 50% tax penalty."
   - Source: behalfkr.com, KOWORK
   - Verified: true

2. **Keep a complete paper trail for fund transfers**
   - "Every won transferred must be traceable. Use only bank wire transfers under your name. Unregulated exchange channels = automatic rejection."
   - Source: behalfkr.com, startcompanykorea.com
   - Verified: true

3. **Virtual offices don't work anymore**
   - "Immigration officers now check for 'authentic' business environments. A real office lease is practically required. Paper companies get flagged."
   - Source: behalfkr.com
   - Verified: true

4. **D-8-4S (Startup Special) is new and easier**
   - "Since November 2024, the Startup Korea Special Visa doesn't require degrees or academic scores. You're evaluated on your idea's feasibility and team capability."
   - Source: koreatechdesk.com, MSS
   - Verified: true

5. **Company registration takes 2-4 weeks including bank account**
   - "Don't expect to walk in and start a company in a day. Budget 2-3 weeks for registration alone, up to 4 with the corporate bank account."
   - Source: startcompanykorea.com, pearsonkorea.com
   - Verified: true

### F-2 Community Tips

1. **Income is the biggest point lever**
   - "Income scores make up 60 out of 170 total points. If your annual salary exceeds ₩40M, you can apply immediately without the 3-year stay requirement."
   - Source: KOWORK, IMMIKOREA
   - Verified: true

2. **Negotiate salary on paper carefully**
   - "Instead of '2.5M salary + 0.5M housing allowance', negotiate a flat 3M salary. Your tax return reflects higher income = more points."
   - Source: yourkorea.life
   - Verified: true

3. **KIIP Level 5 completion gives 10 bonus points**
   - "KIIP (사회통합프로그램) Level 5 gives you 10 extra points. It's free, government-run, and counts more than TOPIK for bonus points."
   - Source: IMMIKOREA, famigo.net
   - Verified: true

4. **Donating blood counts as volunteering hours**
   - "You need 50 hours over 6 occasions for 1 year of volunteering credit. Fun fact: blood donation counts! Every donation = community service hours."
   - Source: partners.shuttledelivery.co.kr
   - Verified: true

5. **Start preparing 2-3 years before you need it**
   - "Many students realize too late they lack the points. TOPIK prep, volunteering, salary negotiations — all take time. Start early."
   - Source: famigo.net
   - Verified: true

### H-1 Community Tips

1. **Book your ARC appointment ASAP after arrival**
   - "There's generally a 1-month wait for immigration office appointments. Book on Day 1 even though you have 90 days to register."
   - Source: britadventures.com
   - Verified: true

2. **Confirm accommodation host will cooperate for ARC**
   - "Before booking long-term accommodation, call the host to confirm they'll provide documents for your ARC application. Not all will."
   - Source: britadventures.com
   - Verified: true

3. **Bank accounts are restricted for first 3 months**
   - "Most Korean banks restrict your account for the first 3 months. You need paychecks to lift the restriction. Bring enough cash or have an alternative way to pay rent."
   - Source: britadventures.com
   - Verified: true

4. **Check national quota early — it's first come, first served**
   - "Each country has a limited number of H-1 visas per year (UK: 5,000, Dutch: 200, French: 2,000, German: unlimited). Apply early."
   - Source: pvtistes.net
   - Verified: true

5. **Korea makes everything more complicated than other WHV countries**
   - "Korea was my 5th working holiday country. Every step here is more long-winded than other places. Budget extra time for everything."
   - Source: britadventures.com (expat blog)
   - Verified: true
