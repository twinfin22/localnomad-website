## Visa Options: Your Path to Taiwan

LocalNomad has detailed visa guides for each option. Here's what you need to know:

### **Digital Nomad Visa** (Most Popular)
The stars have aligned. Launched January 2025, this visa is purpose-built for remote workers. [3]

**Key Facts:**
- **Duration:** 6 months; extendable to 2 years (issued for full 2-year period as of January 2026) [3]
- **Income requirement:** Ages 30+: USD $40,000/year; ages 20-29: USD $20,000/year [3]
- **Proof of funds:** USD $10,000 average bank balance over 6 months [3]
- **Where to apply:** Domestic: Taiwan Immigration Bureau; Overseas: Taiwan representative office
- **What it does NOT include:** NHI (National Health Insurance system), labor insurance, work permit for Taiwan employers
- **Important:** You can work for overseas clients; you cannot legally work for Taiwan companies without additional authorization

**→ Full details:** [Digital Nomad Visa](/en/taiwan/visa/digital-nomad)

### **Gold Card** (The Premium Option)
If you're a software engineer, data scientist, finance professional, manager with 5+ years experience, artist, educator, or other specialist, Taiwan wants you—and they're offering serious perks. [4]

**Key Benefits:**
- **Legal work authorization** for Taiwan employers
- **Access to NHI (National Health Insurance)** (world-class healthcare from day one)
- **Tax incentives:** First 5 years, 50% tax exemption on salary income exceeding NT$3M; overseas income exempt from Alternative Minimum Tax (AMT) calculations [5]
- **Multi-year validity:** Much more stable than short-term visas
- **Banking:** Easy bank account opening

**Who qualifies:** Professionals in priority sectors (tech, finance, management, education, culture). Experience and credentials required. 2026 updates include new fields in healthcare, environmental science, and biotechnology. [4]

**→ Full details:** [Gold Card](/en/taiwan/visa/gold-card)

### **Other Visa Options**
- **Visitor Visa (Tourist):** 60–90 days, visa-free for many nationalities. Gray area for remote work. [6]
- **Employment ARC (Alien Residence Certificate):** Standard work visa if you have a Taiwan employer
- **APRC (Alien Permanent Resident Certificate):** Permanent residency (after holding ARC for certain duration)

**→ Explore all options:** [Taiwan Visa Hub](/en/taiwan/visas)

Price varies by neighborhood (Da'an/Zhongshan more expensive), size, and condition. Expect to spend more in Taipei, less in other cities.

### **6-Month+ Stay (Digital Nomad Visa or Gold Card)**
**Visa:** Digital Nomad Visa (best for pure remote workers) OR Gold Card (if you qualify)
**Accommodation:** 6–12 month lease, unfurnished or semi-furnished apartment
**Budget:** $1,300–1,800 USD/month (rent drops with longer leases)

**Tax note:** After 183 days in Taiwan, you're a tax resident liable for Taiwan income tax on worldwide income. [11] This is not a surprise—it's law. Plan accordingly (consult a tax accountant).

Taiwan uses the New Taiwan Dollar (TWD). Exchange rate as of March 2026: USD 1 ≈ TWD 31.68 [14]

### **Banking**

**Opening an Account:**

Traditional challenge: Banks required work permits or residency permits to open accounts. Good news: As of 2025, major banks (CTBC, Cathay United, E.SUN) now accept Digital Nomad Visa holders—a major shift from previous years. [15]

**Recommended Banks (2025-2026 Update):**
- **CTBC Bank:** Fastest digital growth, "My Way" digital account, good for remote workers; accepts DN Visa
- **Cathay United Bank:** Foreign exchange expertise, multi-currency; accepts DN Visa (major 2025 shift)
- **E.SUN Bank:** International transfer friendly, English app; accepts DN Visa

**What you need:**
- Passport + Taiwan address (can be your Airbnb)
- Digital Nomad Visa (critical: major banks now explicitly accept this as of 2025—check before visiting; major update from previous years)
- Some banks may request: proof of income (bank statements from home country)

**Timeline:** Account opened in 1–2 visits. Call ahead or check bank website for DN Visa account requirements (policies changed March 2025).

### **Mobile Payments**

Physical cash is still common, but digital payments are everywhere:
- **LINE Pay:** Most popular; use at restaurants, convenience stores, shops
- **JKoPay:** Becoming standard
- **Apple Pay / Google Pay:** Works at most terminals

Night market vendors? Still cash. Restaurants? 50/50. Convenience stores? Cards preferred.

### **International Transfers**

**Receiving money from overseas clients:**
1. Open foreign currency deposit account with your Taiwan bank
2. Request SWIFT code
3. Clients send to your SWIFT account
4. Convert to TWD at favorable rates (banks competitive)
5. Timeline: 1–3 business days

**Using Wise:** Supported by all major Taiwan banks. Excellent rates for international transfers.

**Limit:** Personal remittances capped at NT$5 million (USD ~$160K) monthly.

### **Cash**

ATMs are ubiquitous. Most accept international cards (Visa, Mastercard, Union Pay).

**Where cash is essential:**
- Night markets (vendors prefer cash)
- Small family restaurants
- Old-school temples and shops
- Tipping (not customary, but some upscale restaurants accept it)

## Healthcare & Insurance

Taiwan has one of the world's best healthcare systems. Here's how to navigate it as a nomad.

### **National Health Insurance (NHI)**

**For Digital Nomad Visa holders:**
- **Eligibility:** 6-month waiting period required
- **Access:** Can register for NHI (National Health Insurance) after 6 months of residence
- **Cost once eligible:** Percentage-based contribution (5.17% premium rate in 2026)—not a fixed monthly fee, but calculated as a percentage of your insured income [16]
- **Coverage:** Outpatient (doctor visits), inpatient (hospital), dental, prescriptions, traditional Chinese medicine
- **Not included in visa:** Must arrange private insurance for the first 6 months while waiting for NHI eligibility

**For Gold Card holders:**
- **Immediate eligibility:** Can enroll in NHI (National Health Insurance) from day one [16]
- **Huge advantage:** Access to world-class healthcare system immediately without waiting—no gap period without coverage

### **Private Insurance (Mandatory for Digital Nomads)**

**Required for visa:** You must have valid health insurance for your entire visa stay.

**Cost:** $20–50 USD/month (depending on age, coverage level)

**Providers:**
- AXA
- Allianz
- Cigna
- Other international insurers

**Coverage:** Typically includes hospitalization, emergency care, some outpatient. Check exclusions.

**Timeline:** Get quotes, purchase before arrival. Show proof with visa application.

### **Healthcare Quality**

Taiwan's medical system is genuinely excellent:
- **Hospitals:** Modern facilities in all major cities
- **Doctors:** Many speak English in central areas; assume English in rural areas
- **Specialists:** Readily available; shorter wait times than US/Europe
- **Cost (out-of-pocket):** [17]
  - Doctor visit: ~NT$500–1,500 (USD $17–50) without NHI; ~NT$100 copay with NHI
  - Hospital stay: Affordable by Western standards
  - Prescription drugs: Inexpensive (often NT$50–200 per medication)
- **Emergency:** Dial 119 (ambulance), covered by NHI or private insurance

### **Clinic Culture (Walk-In for Everything)**

Walk-in clinics normal practice in Taiwan. No appointments required; accessible clinics everywhere (densest in cities). Many clinics specialize (dermatology, orthopedics, etc.), not just general practice. Hours often extend until 9 PM; weekend hours common. Cost without NHI: NT$500–1,500 copay. Hidden gem: Clinics often faster than hospitals for minor issues; same-day results common.

### **Pharmacies (藥局) — OTC Culture**

Pharmacist-staffed pharmacies everywhere (not just convenience stores). OTC medications (cold medicine, pain relief, allergies) available without prescription. Cost very cheap: NT$20–100 per medication typical. Pharmacist consultation free; pharmacists often speak some English or use Google Translate. Many conditions treated by pharmacists alone (no doctor visit needed). Antibiotics typically require prescription (stricter than Western countries).

### **Cheap Dental & Vision**

- **Dental:** NHI covers most procedures; out-of-pocket NT$200–500 per visit (30–70% cheaper than US/Australia)
- **Vision:** Optometry clinic visits cheap (NT$200–300); glasses NT$1,000–3,000 (US $30–100)
- **Quality:** Standards high; many providers English-friendly in Taipei
- **Insurance:** Plan dental work if staying 6+ months (NHI coverage significant)

### **Traditional Chinese Medicine Covered by NHI (Unique to Taiwan)**

Herbal medicine, acupuncture, tuina (massage) covered by NHI. Cost with NHI: copayment ~NT$100/visit. Without NHI: NT$500–1,500/session. Many clinics integrate both TCM and Western medicine. Hidden gem: Excellent for chronic conditions (back pain, etc.); many nomads swear by it as alternative to Western pain management.

### **Practical Timeline**

| Timeline | Action |
|---|---|
| **Before arrival** | Purchase private health insurance |
| **Weeks 1–2** | Keep receipts if you need medical care (private insurance reimburses) |
| **Month 2–3** | Register for NHI application (requires ARC) |
| **Month 6** | NHI becomes active; switch to national system |
| **Month 6+** | Use NHI for ongoing care (percentage-based contribution) |

## Getting Around

Taiwan's transportation is cheap, clean, and efficient. Your EasyCard is your best friend.

### **EasyCard: The Backbone**

EasyCard is a reusable transit card that works everywhere in Taiwan. Think of it like London's Oyster card or Hong Kong's Octopus. Buy once, use forever.

**Cost:** NT$100 (just card) to NT$500 (card + pre-loaded credit) [8]
- Base card: NT$100 (non-refundable, but you keep the card forever)
- Common package: NT$500 total (NT$100 card + NT$400 pre-loaded credit you can spend)

**Works on:**
- Taipei MRT (Mass Rapid Transit subway system)
- Buses throughout Taiwan (long-distance and local)
- Convenience stores (7-Eleven, FamilyMart) for food, drinks, snacks, bill payments
- Parking meters and vending machines (hidden gem—saves change-hunting)
- Most restaurant chains
- Bike-sharing (YouBike)
- Some taxis (not mandatory, but many accept it)

**Auto-Load Setup:** Co-branded cards linked to credit/debit accounts enable automatic refill. If staying 3+ months, request auto-load setup when buying the card—you'll never run out of balance mid-journey.

**Refund:** Return card with any remaining balance to station kiosk; the balance is refunded (card deposit of NT$100 is non-refundable, but you keep the reusable card forever for next visit).

### **MRT (Mass Rapid Transit / Taipei Subway)**

Clean, fast, crowded during rush hours (7–9 AM, 5–7 PM).

**Fares:** NT$20–60 depending on distance traveled

**Coverage:** Extensive in Taipei; expanding systems in Taichung and Kaohsiung

**Frequency:** Trains every 2–4 minutes during peak times, every 5–10 minutes off-peak (excellent reliability)

**Transfers:** EasyCard gives NT$8 discount when transferring between MRT and bus within 1 hour of first boarding

**TPASS (Monthly Pass):** NT$1,200/month covers unlimited travel on Taipei City MRT, buses, some ferries across Taipei City, New Taipei City, Keelung, and Taoyuan (including Airport MRT to downtown). Worth it if you travel frequently between areas or take long-distance trips weekly. [18]

### **Buses — Critical Skill: Press Button to Stop**

Taiwan has extensive and affordable bus networks in every city. Challenge: Route names and stops are in Mandarin Chinese, which is difficult for non-speakers. Solution: Use Google Maps (enter destination, it shows bus options in English transliteration). Buses accept EasyCard for payment.

**Hidden gem (critical skill):** Drivers will NOT stop unless you press the stop button. Button typically located near doors or above windows. Press once you see your stop approaching (1–2 blocks before). This is essential—many novice riders miss stops because they don't signal.

## Eating Well: Food Culture

This might be Taiwan's greatest superpower. The food is incredible, cheap, and everywhere.

### **Night Markets (夜市): Taiwan's Crown Jewel**

Night markets are evening street food and shopping festivals found in every Taiwanese city. They open around 6–7 PM and run until 11 PM or midnight. Entire streets are blocked off for food stalls, game booths, and vendors. This is where Taiwanese families, friends, and locals gather to socialize and eat cheap, incredible food. It's not just food—it's the social and cultural heart of Taiwan.

**Typical dishes:** [20]
- **Gua bao** (pork belly bun): NT$40–80 (USD $1.50–3)
- **Braised pork rice:** NT$30–60 (USD $1–2)
- **Oyster omelets:** NT$60–100 (USD $2–3)
- **Stinky tofu:** NT$60–100 (USD $2–3)
- **Bubble tea:** NT$30–80 (USD $1–2.70)
- **Grilled seafood:** NT$100–200+ (USD $3–7)
- **Crepes:** NT$80–150 (USD $2.70–5)

**Cost:** Typical meal = NT$150–300 (USD $5–10) to try multiple items

**Famous markets:**
- **Shilin** (Taipei): Biggest, most touristy, good for atmosphere
- **Raohe** (Taipei): Smaller, locals prefer it
- **Liuhe** (Kaohsiung): Excellent seafood focus
- **Nantai** (Taichung): Modern, well-organized

**Vibe:** Social gathering spot, not just food. Families, friends, couples, solo eaters. Everyone mixes.

**Ordering Etiquette (Practical Tips):**
- **Non-verbal communication:** Pointing with index finger is 100% normal and expected—don't feel awkward
- **Quantity:** Hold up fingers (1, 2, 3) to indicate how many
- **Queue culture:** Orderly; locals queue patiently. Nobody browses ahead of ordering. Respect the line.
- **Speed:** Speak clearly and quickly once ordering; vendors move fast
- **Payment:** Pay immediately after receiving food unless vendor indicates otherwise

### **Breakfast Shops (早餐店) — Uniquely Taiwanese**

This is a cultural experience unique to Taiwan. Every neighborhood has standalone breakfast-only shops (5:30–10 AM typical).

**Popular items:**
- Egg and cheese crepes
- Rice burgers (fan tuan) with egg, meat, pickled vegetables
- Youtiao (fried dough sticks) dipped in soy milk
- Fresh-squeezed orange juice
- Steamed buns with various fillings

**Ordering:** Say 內用 (eat here) or 外帶 (take away); busy shops have separate lines. Self-seating at casual breakfast shops (unlike traditional restaurants).

**Hidden gem:** Breakfast cheapest meal of day (NT$40–80 full meal). Become a regular; staff remembers your order after 2–3 visits. This is local culture—join in.

## Taxes & Legal

This is the part nobody wants to talk about. Let's talk about it.

### **Tax Residency: The 183-Day Rule**

**Critical:** If you spend 183+ days in Taiwan in a calendar year, you become a tax resident liable for Taiwan income tax. [11]

**What this means:**
- You owe Taiwan income tax on **worldwide income**—not just money earned in Taiwan, but all your remote work income (from any client or employer globally)
- This is mandatory and non-negotiable
- The Digital Nomad Visa does NOT exempt you from taxes (no visa type exempts you)
- It applies equally to everyone: tourist visa, digital nomad visa, gold card

**Timeline example:** If you arrive January 1 and stay until June 22, that's 183 days—you are a tax resident for that full year and owe Taiwan tax on worldwide income.

**Action:** If planning to stay longer than 6 months, consult a Taiwan tax accountant BEFORE arrival to plan your tax strategy.

**Planning questions to discuss with a Taiwan tax accountant:**
- Does your home country have a double taxation treaty with Taiwan? (If yes, you may avoid double taxation)
- Should you work through a holding company or business entity for tax efficiency?
- Can you offset foreign tax credits you've already paid at home?
- How should you structure your invoicing (personal vs. business)?

**Penalty for not filing:** Taiwan takes this seriously. File correctly.

### **Tax Rates (2025)**

Taiwan's progressive tax system. General rates (not including surtax):
- First NT$300,000: 5%
- NT$300K–600K: 12%
- NT$600K–1M: 20%
- NT$1M–2M: 30%
- Above NT$2M: 40%

(Simplified; actual calculation more complex. Consult accountant.)

### **Gold Card Tax Benefits**

**Special treatment:** Gold Card holders get favorable tax treatment. [5]

**Tax incentive period:** Five years from start of employment in Taiwan. Example: If you start working for a Taiwan company in 2026, the tax benefits apply through end of 2030. [5]

**Example benefits:**
- 50% exemption on annual salary income exceeding NT$3M (e.g., if you earn NT$5M, only NT$2M is taxed)
- Overseas income (from foreign clients) is exempt from Alternative Minimum Tax (AMT) calculations [5]

**This changes everything:** If you qualify for Gold Card, it's not just a visa—it's a tax-advantaged path to residency. Seriously consider applying.

### **Working on Visa-Free Entry**

Some nomads work on 90-day visa-free entry (entry with no visa required for eligible nationalities). [6]

**Legal status:** Gray area. The Visitor Visa requirements say "unrelated to paid employment," but the definition is vague and enforcement is unclear. The government hasn't explicitly said "work is forbidden" but also hasn't said "work is allowed."

**Risk:** Theoretically, immigration could request you leave if they determine you're working. This is not guaranteed to happen, but it's a risk.

**Better approach:** Use Digital Nomad Visa instead. It's specifically designed for remote workers, costs roughly USD $200–300, and gives you explicit legal authorization to work for overseas clients.

### **Important Restrictions**

- **Can't work for Taiwan companies** on Digital Nomad Visa without an additional work authorization permit. If you want to work for a Taiwan employer, you need a different visa (Employment ARC or Gold Card).
- **Can work for overseas clients** (your typical remote work situation—freelancing, contractor work for foreign companies)
- **No work permit included** in DN Visa—remote work for overseas clients is explicitly allowed, but you can't legally work for Taiwan companies
- **Must have health insurance** (not included in visa; your responsibility to purchase private coverage)
- **No NHI access** until 6 months of residence (plan private insurance for the first 6 months)

### **Typhoon Preparedness**

**Typhoon season:** July–October (peak damage: August–September). Taiwan averages 3.5 typhoons/year. [21]

**Official warning system:** Schools/businesses close automatically based on typhoon alert level. Automatic closure system called "Typhoon Days Off" = work/school announces closure based on official warnings (varies by region; some districts lenient).

**What happens:**
- Heavy rain, strong winds
- Schools and businesses close during strong typhoons
- Flooding in low-lying areas
- Flight cancellations

**What you should do:**
- Have insurance that covers typhoon damage
- Stock supplies (water, food, medications) 24 hours before expected arrival
- Stay indoors during active typhoon warnings
- Monitor weather; it's not a surprise (weeks of warning)

**Reality:** Typhoons are manageable, not apocalyptic. Life continues with disruptions. Hidden gem: Typhoons bring timely rainfall for forests/crops; often mild despite forecasts.

## Unique Taiwan Experiences & Hidden Gems

### **Garbage Truck Culture (YES, You Chase the Truck!)**

One of Taiwan's most uniquely charming rituals.

**The system:**
- Collection schedule: 5 times weekly in Taipei (no collection Wednesday, Sunday)
- Iconic music: Trucks play Beethoven's "Für Elise" or "A Maiden's Prayer" as alert
- Process: Take trash to truck stop when music plays; no street-side collection
- Apps: Download "台北倒垃圾" (iPhone) or "Taipei Garbage Truck Map" (Android) for schedule + real-time truck locations

**Waste sorting (3-category system):**
1. **General waste:** Must use official blue Taipei bags (NT$8–20/bag)
2. **Recycling:** Free; must be clean, flattened, or prepared (electronics separate)
3. **Food waste:** Two categories—raw (compost) vs. cooked (animal feed)

**Hidden gem:** This community ritual = opportunity to meet neighbors. Locals gather at collection points; show respect by sorting correctly (locals may judge foreign mistakes jokingly). Missing the truck = illegal to dump trash elsewhere; must keep until next collection.

### **Hot Springs (溫泉) Culture**

Taiwan ranked top 15 globally; highest density/variety of hot springs in world.

**Availability:** Almost every county (except Changhua, Yunlin, Penghu) has hot springs. Types: hot springs, cold springs, mud springs, seabed hot springs.

**Notable locations:**
- Beitou (Taipei): Accessible by MRT
- Jiaoxi (Yilan): "Beauty Soup" waters
- Guanziling (Tainan): Rejuvenating mud springs
- Green Island: Seawater hot springs

**Cost:** Public hot spring areas cheap (NT$100–300 entry); hot spring resorts pricey. Hidden gem: Outdoor/wild hot springs = free but require hiking/local knowledge.

**2025–2026 tourism theme:** "Hot Spring Island Tours" integrating 19 major regions with local cuisines, hiking trails, cultural experiences.

### **Hiking Culture (台灣百岳)**

**台灣百岳:** 100 peaks over 3,000m; iconic hiking challenge.

**Accessibility:** Mountain trails throughout Taiwan; various difficulty levels. Many peaks accessible day-trips from cities (e.g., Elephant Mountain = 30 min from central Taipei).

**Best season:** October–November (cooler); avoid summer (typhoon risk) and winter.

**Culture:** Hiking groups active; easy to join; popular weekend activity. Hidden gem: Hiking community welcoming; many meet-ups for beginners. Mountains safe; trail markers clear; thousands hike daily.

### **Tea Culture**

Taiwanese tea serious business: oolong (light/heavy roast), high-mountain (fragrant, delicate), bubble tea (casual).

**Tea houses:** Traditional shops teach brewing methods; social experience. Quality tea expensive but worth (NT$200–500+ per session). Hidden gem: Learning tea ceremony = cultural window into Taiwan's sophistication. Locals appreciate foreigners showing genuine interest.
