## Visa Options: Summary + Links

### F-1-D: Digital Nomad Visa (Workation Visa)
**Best for:** Remote workers, freelancers, online entrepreneurs earning ₩88.1M+ ($66K) annually. [1]

Up to **2 years** (1 year + 1-year renewal). [1] Must demonstrate insurance coverage (₩100M / ~$75K) [1] and comply with filing requirements. Published guidelines indicate this is Korea's longest digital nomad visa in East Asia. Check [F-1-D Visa Details](/en/korea/visa/F-1-D) for application requirements and income thresholds.

### B-2: Short-Term Tourism Visa
**Best for:** Testing the waters, 1–3 month trips, visa-free entry available for many nationalities.

90-day visa-free entry for citizens of 106+ countries. [2] Extensions to 180 days possible. Ideal for first-time visitors. See [B-2 Visa Details](/en/korea/visa/B-2) for your nationality's specific timeline.

### D-2: Student Visa (with work authorization)
**Best for:** Language learners, degree-seeking students who want part-time remote work permitted.

Allows up to 20 hours/week off-campus employment. Published guidelines indicate this requires enrollment in an accredited Korean language or university program. See [D-2 Visa Details](/en/korea/visa/D-2).

### E-1: English Teaching Visa
**Best for:** Native English speakers doing English instruction (can mix with remote work via F-1-D intent).

1–2 year contract-based. See [E-1 Visa Details](/en/korea/visa/E-1) for sponsorship requirements.

## First 72 Hours Checklist

### Hour 0–2: Airport Arrival (Incheon or Gimpo, Seoul)

- **Get SIM/eSIM & internet:**
  - Buy prepaid SIM at airport kiosk (SKT, KT, LG U+) — ₩30K–₩50K for 30-day plan (varies by data: 15GB ₩30K, 30GB ₩40K, unlimited ₩50K)
  - eSIM alternative: QR code activation, no SIM card to physically swap; same pricing
  - Test: Open Naver Map or Kakao Map immediately to verify connectivity

- **Get T-Money card:**
  - Convenience stores in airport (CU, GS25) — ₩3,000 card fee (standard price; ₩4,000 at airport due to convenience markup) + load with ₩20K–₩50K balance [4]
  - Universal payment: Metro (subways), buses, bike rentals, and most convenience stores accept T-Money

- **Airport transport to accommodation:**
  - **Limousine Bus** (direct service, most nomad-friendly): ₩10K–₩15K. Goes directly to major districts (Hongdae, Gangnam, Myeongdong, etc.) without stops. 40–60 minutes. Book window/counter inside airport.
  - **Airport Coach Bus** (budget option, shared stops): ₩6K–₩10K. Shared route with multiple stops around Seoul. Slower than limousine but cheaper. Same payment window.
  - **Subway**: Incheon Line connects airport directly; ₩4,150–₩4,750 (All-Stop Train) or ₩9,500 (Express Train), ~43–60 minutes to central Seoul [3]. Cheapest option, but requires navigating metro with luggage.
  - **Taxi**: ₩60K–₩80K depending on destination (highway tolls + fuel surcharge apply). Most expensive but fastest for single passengers.

### Hour 2–6: Get to Accommodation & Settle In

- **Change clothes, rest 1–2 hours** — jet lag management
- **Charge all devices** — locate outlet (220V, Type C/F plugs)
- **Locate nearest convenience store** — CU or GS25 for basics, drinks, snacks
- **Register SIM, test mobile data** — confirm KakaoTalk, messaging apps work
- **Scout neighborhood:**
  - Walk to nearest metro station (note exit number)
  - Identify convenience store, pharmacy (약국 — where you buy non-prescription meds + vitamins), restaurant
  - Download Papago (translation app) for offline use

### Day 1–2: Banking, Address Registration, Essentials

- **Open a bank account** (if staying 3+ months):
  - Visit nearest Hana Bank, Shinhan, or KB Kookmin branch
  - Bring: Passport, ARC (Alien Registration Card — if you have one), Korean phone number
  - Takes 20–30 minutes; SMS verification required
  - Recommended: Hana Bank (best FX rates), Shinhan Bank (modern app), or Toss Bank (neo-bank, fully digital)

- **Register address** (if staying 6+ months):
  - Go to nearest **주민센터 (Jumincentre — local district office)** — one exists in every Seoul neighborhood
  - Bring: Passport, rental contract or lease document, proof of residence
  - Required for NHIS (National Health Insurance Service — see Healthcare section) enrollment

- **Purchase essentials:**
  - Toiletries (if not in accommodation)
  - Phone charger + power bank
  - Comfortable walking shoes (you'll walk a lot)
  - Any prescription medications (customs: bring copies of prescriptions)

### Day 2–3: Apps, Delivery, Explore Neighborhood

- **Download essential apps** (iOS/Android — all free):
  - **Naver Map or Kakao Map**: Real-time navigation + restaurant/cafe reviews with ratings (must-have for getting around)
  - **Baemin or Coupang Eats**: Food delivery apps — order from thousands of restaurants, delivery in 30–45 minutes (₩4K–₩8K per order)
  - **Kakao T**: Taxi hailing app — shows transparent pricing upfront before booking (much cheaper than calling yellow taxis)
  - **Coupang**: Same-day shopping + grocery delivery (if you have account/address registered)
  - **KakaoTalk**: Korea's primary messaging app — everyone uses it for communication, not optional (like WhatsApp in Europe)
  - **Papago**: Naver's AI translation app (works offline in basic mode for camera + text translation)
  - **Banking app**: Hana, Shinhan, or your chosen bank (for balance checks, transfers, no fees)

- **Order first meal via delivery:**
  - Open Baemin, search "라면" (Ramen — instant noodles, comfort food) or "김밥" (Kimbap — rice + vegetables wrapped in seaweed, sushi-like)
  - Set delivery address, pay via card or digital wallet
  - Tip: Always check estimated delivery time before ordering

- **Walk your neighborhood:**
  - Find favorite coffee spot (카페 — café; Korea has strong café culture) — most have free WiFi and welcome laptop work with small coffee purchase
  - Identify E-mart or Homeplus (large supermarket chains like Costco; cheap for bulk groceries)
  - Note: Subway exits have neighborhood maps; screenshot them

## Stay Duration: 1-Month, 3-Month, 6-Month Plans

### 1-Month Stay: Tourist Mode

**Visa:** B-2 visa-free entry (90-day visa-free for most nationalities)

**Accommodation:**
- Airbnb, hotel monthlies, or coliving day rates
- Budget: ₩500K–₩1M ($385–$770)
- Booking: 2–3 weeks in advance for best availability

**Setup minimalism:**
- SIM card only (no bank account needed)
- Google Pay / Apple Pay for contactless payments
- Carry some cash (₩50K–₩100K) for emergency

**Daily rhythm:**
- Cafe coworking (free WiFi, ₩3K–₩5K coffee expected for 2–3 hour stay)
- Takeout or restaurant meals (no need to cook)
- Explore 1–2 neighborhoods each weekend

**Success criteria:**
- Internet tested & stable
- Found 2–3 preferred coworking cafes
- Familiar with subway + T-Money card usage

---

### 3-Month Stay: Settling In Phase

**Visa:** B-2 extension to 180 days OR apply for F-1-D (if income-eligible)

**Accommodation:**
- Airbnb, shared house, coliving, or short-term lease
- Budget: ₩600K–₩900K ($460–$690)
- Tip: Negotiate 3-month discount (10–15% typical)

**Housing App Comparison (Zigbang vs Dabang):**
- **Zigbang:** Most listings (unlimited), but many fake listings (must verify with agent/visit in person)
- **Dabang:** Higher quality, fewer fakes, better filtering by budget/features, location-first search (shows best options in chosen area first), higher user satisfaction
- **Nomad Hack:** Use Dabang first for curated listings, then cross-check on Zigbang for additional options

**Bank account:**
- Open now (even if not earning in Korea)
- Enables local payments, direct deposit alternatives

**Phone plan upgrade:**
- If staying, consider postpaid plan: ₩20K–₩40K/month with contract
- More stable than prepaid; better rates

**Community integration:**
- Join the LocalNomad Community for meetups and local tips
- Attend coworking space orientation
- Follow local expat Facebook groups for housing/job tips

**Tax/Legal consideration:**
- Not yet tax resident (under 183 days)
- Keep records of departure/entry dates

**Success criteria:**
- Bank account open
- Favorite neighborhood identified
- At least 1 in-person nomad connection made

### 6-Month+ Stay: Long-Term Mode

**Visa:** F-1-D Digital Nomad Visa (strongly recommended) OR extended D-2/F-2

**Accommodation:**
- Lease apartment (전세 = long-term lease with large deposit, 월세 = month-to-month rent), coliving, or shared house
- Budget: ₩700K–₩1.2M ($540–$920)
- Process: 2–3 week search + viewing cycle; deposit structures vary (typical 월세 requires ₩500K–₩2M deposit + monthly rent)

**Officetel (오피스텔) Culture:**
- Mixed-use building with residential + commercial spaces, mostly rented as apartments
- Located near subways, offices, universities (excellent for commute)
- Modern, well-maintained, clean, amenities in same building (convenience store, restaurants)
- Good for short-term + long-term leases
- Even studios ask ₩5M deposits (refundable if legal contract registered properly)

**Nomad Deposit Negotiation Hack (Lesser-Known):**
- **Inverse relationship:** Higher deposit ↔ Lower monthly rent
- **Strategy:** If you can afford higher upfront deposit (via bank transfer), landlords often drop monthly rent ₩100K–₩300K
- **Example:** Instead of ₩2M deposit + ₩600K/month → negotiate to ₩5M deposit + ₩500K/month (saves ₩100K/month = ₩1.2M/year)
- **Advantage:** Deposit is fully refundable if contract is legal; monthly savings compound over lease period

**Financial setup:**
- Bank account open & tested
- Health insurance enrollment (NHIS — National Health Insurance Service) mandatory at 6+ months
  - Cost: ₩110K–₩130K/month (~$85–$100) [5]
  - Coverage: 60–70% of medical expenses (hospital, doctor visits, prescriptions, preventive care) [5]
- Consider tax accountant engagement (~₩200K–₩500K/year for bookkeeping)

**Visa documentation:**
- F-1-D requires: Proof of income (₩88.1M/$66K annually), insurance coverage (₩100M/$75K), ARC registration
- Published guidelines indicate: Extensions possible year-by-year

**Tax residency awareness:**
- At 183+ calendar days, you become tax resident
- Subject to Korean income tax on worldwide earnings (6–42% progressive rates)
- Consult tax advisor about double taxation treaties (US, Canada, UK, Australia have treaties with Korea)

**Lifestyle establishment:**
- Regular coworking space membership (₩180K–₩500K/month depending on premium)
- Monthly meetups, sports clubs, hobby groups
- Established restaurant/cafe circuit
- Part-time freelance work or side gigs (permitted under F-1-D intent)

**Success criteria:**
- NHIS enrollment complete
- Stable apartment secured
- Tax advisor consulted
- Local community integration meaningful

## City Guides: Seoul, Busan, Jeju

### Seoul: The Digital Nomad Hub

**Vibe:**
Seoul is bustling, 24-hour, relentlessly modern with pockets of tradition. World-class infrastructure, nightlife, food, and the largest nomad community in Korea make it ideal for those seeking connection and energy. Expect crowds, noise, and an exhausting pace if you're not prepared.

**Best Neighborhoods:**

#### Hongdae (弘大) — Budget + Bohemian
- **Rent:** ₩600K–₩900K studio/1BR ($460–$690)
- **Vibe:** Young professionals, artists, indie shops, street art, late-night cafes
- **Suited for:** Budget-conscious nomads, creatives, social butterflies
- **Pros:** Affordable, vibrant nightlife, café culture, coworking accessible
- **Cons:** Busy weekends (noise), touristy in parts, petty street crime (rare)
- **Foreigner density:** Moderate to high

#### Itaewon (이태원) — Expat Central
- **Rent:** ₩800K–₩1.2M studio/1BR ($615–$920)
- **Vibe:** International, expat-heavy, nightlife district, diverse food, business district edge
- **Suited for:** Social/dating-focused nomads, those seeking English-speaking communities
- **Pros:** English widely spoken, expat bars/restaurants, coworking abundant, nightlife
- **Cons:** Higher rent, touristy, occasional petty crime (theft, harassment), can feel less "Korean"
- **Foreigner density:** Very high

#### Gangnam (강남) — Luxury + Corporate
- **Rent:** ₩1M–₩1.5M studio/1BR ($770–$1.15K)
- **Vibe:** Fast-paced, corporate, luxury shopping, fine dining, expensive

- **Pros:** Premium amenities, coworking abundance, business networking, safe

- **Rent:** ₩550K–₩800K studio/1BR ($420–$615)
- **Vibe:** Mixed nightlife/residential, cafes, local feel, central location
- **Suited for:** Mid-budget nomads, those wanting balance of price and location
- **Pros:** Cheap, central, local vibe, coworking accessible

### Jeju Island (제주도 — Jeju-Do): Beach + Tourist Visa

**Vibe:**
Jeju is a tropical island escape — Korea's "Hawaii." Stunning natural scenery, relaxed atmosphere, popular with tourists and visa-free explorers. 10–15% more expensive than Seoul due to island shipping logistics. Best as a short-term (1–3 month) retreat, not for long-term residence (limited nomad community, fewer coworking spaces).

**Accommodation:**
- Limited long-term rental data in 2026 guides; typically seasonal pricing
- High during summer (June–August): ₩800K–₩1.5M
- Low during winter (December–February): ₩500K–₩800K
- Airbnb heavily available; negotiate longer-term discounts

**Suited for:**
- Beach-loving remote workers
- Those on tourist visas wanting scenic backdrop
- Seasonal workers, temporary retreats

**Best months:** Spring (April–May) and fall (September–October) for weather + fewer crowds

**Considerations:**
- Fewer coworking spaces; cafe working recommended

## Healthcare & Insurance

### National Health Insurance (NHIS)

**Eligibility:** Stay 6+ months with valid ARC → Mandatory enrollment within 2 weeks of registration

**Cost:** ₩110K–₩130K per month (~$85–$100)

**Coverage:** 60–70% of expenses
- Hospital visits
- Outpatient care
- Prescription medications
- Emergency services
- Preventive care & checkups
- Dental (routine cleaning, extractions covered; cosmetic/implants not)
- Vision (basic exams + glasses covered; LASIK/SMILE not covered)

**Enrollment:** Visit local health insurance office (건강보험공단) with ARC; takes 1 hour

**Important:** Premiums based on income; retroactively adjusted annual; keep records of paychecks or self-employment income.

### Private Insurance Alternatives (If Staying < 6 months or Want Premium Coverage)

- **AXA Korea:** Expat-friendly, comprehensive, ~₩120K–₩200K/month
- **Mercer:** International coverage, commonly used
- **Cost:** Higher than NHIS but often cover services NHIS doesn't (cosmetic, alternative medicine)

### Getting Meds Without a Doctor Visit (Pharmacy Hacks)

**How Korean Pharmacies (약국) Work:**
- Pharmacists recommend medicine based on symptoms (not like Western "browse shelves" system)
- NO doctor visit required for common medicines
- Pharmacists trained in symptom-to-medicine matching
- No appointment needed; walk-in welcome

**Communication Hack:**
- **Papago app** (Korean translation app) → Type/speak symptoms → Show translation to pharmacist
- Works especially well for medical terms (better than Google Translate for Korean medical language)
- Example: "감기" (감기 = cold) → pharmacist immediately understands

**OTC Meds Available Without Doctor:**
- Common cold remedies, ibuprofen, acetaminophen, digestive aids, topical creams
- Birth control (OTC, no prescription needed — major advantage for women nomads)
- Acne treatments (Acnon is TikTok-viral Korean acne cream, ₩5K–₩10K)
- Allergy meds, sleeping aids, vitamin supplements

**Finding Pharmacies:**
- Open Naver Maps → Search "약국" → Shows all nearby with hours + reviews
- Usually within 100m of any subway station

**Nomad Advantage:** Dental + dermatology tourism (world-class Korean specialists at 50–70% cheaper than US/UK prices; no appointment needed for walk-in consultations)

### High-Speed Rail: KTX vs SRT

**SRT (Super Rapid Train)** — Nomad Hack
- **Originates from:** Suseo Station (Gangnam — convenient if staying there)
- **Speed:** 305 km/h, slightly faster (Seoul → Busan in 2h 30min)
- **Cost:** ₩51,800 (~$39) — CHEAPER than KTX

- **Best for:** Gangnam nomads, budget-conscious (faster + cheaper combo)

- **Seating:** Economy + First-class options available
- **Best for:** North/West connections, prefer more comfort/options

**Nomad Money-Saving Hack:**
If in Gangnam, take SRT instead of KTX: saves ₩8,000–₩13,000 per trip + 15–30 min faster. Worth the Suseo Station detour.

**Popular routes:**
- **Seoul → Busan:** KTX ₩59,800, SRT ₩51,800; trains every 15–30 minutes [8]
- **Seoul → Gwangju:** KTX ₩49,200 (~$38)
- **Booking:** Online via Korail website (www.letskorail.com) or Naver Train app; open 30 days in advance

## Taxes & Legal: Residency, Income, Visa Rules

### 183-Day Tax Residency Rule [21]

**What triggers it:** If you spend 183+ cumulative days in Korea during a calendar year (January–December), you're classified as a tax resident. This applies whether you're working for Korean companies or earning remotely from overseas clients.

**What happens then (Implications):**
- You become subject to Korean income tax on **all worldwide earnings** — not just Korea income [21]
- You must file an annual tax return by May 31 (reporting prior year's income)
- You pay progressive tax rates: 6–42% depending on total income level [21]
- Plus local tax (~1–2% additional)

**Example:** If you earn $80K from a US client while tax resident in Korea, you owe Korean income tax on that $80K, not just on Korean-earned income.

### Tax Brackets for Residents (2026)

| Income (USD) | Income (KRW) | Rate |
|--------------|--------------|------|
| 0–$31K | 0–₩40.2M | 6% |
| $31K–$70K | ₩40.2M–₩90.9M | 15% |
| $70K–$140K | ₩90.9M–₩181.8M | 24% |
| $140K–$200K | ₩181.8M–₩260M | 35% |
| $200K+ | ₩260M+ | 42% |

**Note:** These are marginal rates; actual tax lower due to progressive brackets. Plus local tax (~1–2%).

### Double Taxation Treaties

Korea has tax treaties with 90+ countries, including:

| Country | Treaty Status | Key Benefit |
|---------|---------------|------------|
| **USA** | Yes | Foreign Tax Credit; prevents double taxation |
| **Canada** | Yes | Comprehensive; consult advisor |
| **UK** | Yes | UK-Korea treaty in effect |
| **Australia** | Yes | Standard provisions |
| **Germany, France, Japan, etc.** | Yes | Vary by treaty; consult advisor |

**Reality:** Double taxation treaty means you don't pay tax twice on the same income, BUT you still owe tax in one country (usually where you earned it or where you're resident). The treaty helps you get a Foreign Tax Credit if you paid tax in one country, so you don't pay again in the other. You still owe taxes — the treaty just prevents the double-bill.

### Remote Work on Tourist Visa: The Gray Area

**The legal situation:**
- Tourist visa (B-2) technically prohibits "work" in Korea
- But "work" legally means working for a Korean employer or earning Korean-source income
- Remote work from overseas clients is legally ambiguous — are you "working in Korea" if your employer and clients are abroad?

**Enforcement reality:**
- Immigration enforcement focuses on: visa overstays, illegal employment (working for Korean companies), and visa abuse
- Remote workers are lower enforcement priority
- However, immigration can refuse entry or fine if they determine you're "working" on tourism visa

**Practical guidance:**
- Under 3 months on tourist visa: Low risk; common for digital nomads
- 3–6 months: Moderate risk if you draw attention (e.g., getting coworking membership, being obvious about it)
- 6+ months: High risk; strongly apply for F-1-D (digital nomad visa) instead — it's designed for this and costs minimal extra effort

**Why F-1-D matters:** You get legal protection for remote work, multi-year extensions, and no tax residency ambiguity.

**Mandatory if:** Staying 90+ days

**Process:**
- Applied via immigration office or employer
- Requires address proof, employment letter (F-1-D: income/insurance docs)
- Takes 1–2 weeks

### Do's & Don'ts for Visa Compliance

**DO:**
- ✅ Keep records of entry/exit dates (automated at airport)
- ✅ Get health insurance if staying 6+ months (mandatory NHIS)
- ✅ Report address changes to immigration
- ✅ Pay taxes if resident (183+ days)
- ✅ Maintain visa documentation (approval notice, ARC)

**DON'T:**

- ❌ Use Jeju visa-free entry to reset B-2 timer — i.e., leaving Korea and re-entering Jeju to get another 90-day tourist visa extension. Immigration now tracks exits/re-entries; doing this results in deportation or fines (~140,000 cases in 2024). [23]

**Seasonal Hacks & Hidden Challenges:**

**Cherry Blossom Season (Late March – Early April)**
- **South-to-North Progression:** Jeju/Busan (late March) → Seoul/North (early April) — predictable, plan accordingly
- **Peak Bloom Window:** Only 4–7 days of true peak bloom per location (book accommodations 2+ months ahead)
- **Fine Dust Risk:** March–April is also hwangsa (yellow dust from China/Central Asia deserts) — air quality often poor; download AirKorea app for daily forecasts
- **Smart Planning:** Have indoor activities ready (museums, galleries, tea cafes) as backup if dust hits

**Monsoon Season (장마 — Late June to Late July)**
- **Pattern:** Short, heavy showers with clear-sky intervals (not all-day rain)
- **Peak:** July is wettest month
- **Prep:** Lightweight rain jacket, umbrella (cheap ponchos at convenience stores for ₩5K)
- **Activity Pivot:** Museums, shopping centers, indoor cafes are busy during rain days (book ahead or work from study cafe)
- **Flooding Risk:** Rare but possible in basements/ground-floor apartments; check before signing lease

**Fine Dust / Yellow Dust (미세먼지 — Miseldunji)**
- **Worst Months:** March–April (desert dust from China/Central Asia)
- **Symptom:** Hazy sky, reduced visibility, respiratory irritation
- **Protection:** Buy KF94 masks in bulk (₩1K–₩3K each, available at all convenience stores)
- **Apps:** AirKorea (daily index) or Airvisual (real-time)
- **Nomad Plan:** Move outdoor activities indoors on poor air quality days (no penalty, just adapt)

**Winter Heating & Ondol Culture (온돌)**
- **Korean heating:** Underfloor heating (radiant heat from below) — completely different from hot air vents
- **Advantage:** Cozy, full-room warmth, no dry air from vents
- **Challenge:** Older apartments may not be well-insulated; landlords may skimp on heating costs
- **Nomad Hack:** Test heat before signing lease, negotiate ondol-equipped apartment (newer buildings standard)
- **Packing:** Heavy sweaters, thermal layers (even with ondol, apartments can feel chilly)

### Packing by Season

#### Spring (April–May)
- Light jacket, sweater (mornings cold, afternoons warm)

#### Autumn (September–November)
