# Japan Visa System Research Report (2024-2026)

**Date**: March 2026
**Source**: Comprehensive web research from Ministry of Foreign Affairs, Immigration Services Agency, and visa processing sources
**Purpose**: Support LocalNomad visa information website with accurate, current data on Japan residence statuses

---

## Executive Summary

Japan's visa system for foreign workers is rapidly evolving, with significant policy changes in 2024-2026. The system is administered by the **Immigration Services Agency (出入国在留管理庁)** under the Ministry of Justice. Key trends include:

- **3.77 million** foreign residents in Japan (end of 2024) — 10.5% annual growth
- Major expansion of **Specified Skilled Worker (SSW)** visa (282,845 increase over 5 years)
- New **Digital Nomad Visa** (6-month, launched 2024)
- Stricter **permanent residency requirements** (mandatory 5-year visa hold as of Feb 2026)
- Introduction of **Employment for Skill Development (ESD)** system replacing Technical Intern Training in 2027
- Fee increases: visa fees rising 5-10× in FY2026

## Part 2: Major Visa Types - Detailed Specifications

### 1. Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services Visa

**Japanese Name**: 技術・人文知識・国際業務

**Category**: Work visa (most popular for white-collar professionals)

#### Requirements

**Education:**
- Bachelor's degree in relevant field (4-year university)
- OR ~10+ years professional experience in field (without degree)
- Degree subject must match job duties (verified via transcripts/graduation certificate)

**Employment:**
- Full-time employment contract with Japanese employer
- Job duties must align with stated qualifications
- Employer must sponsor visa application

**Salary:**
- No minimum stated, but must be comparable to Japanese employees in same role
- Strong financial stability required

**Language:**
- Japanese language NOT mandatory (many tech companies hire English speakers)
- English proficiency often required for international services roles

#### Duration & Extensions

- **Initial stay**: 1, 3, or 5 years (employer and qualifications determine length)
- **Extensions**: Renewable indefinitely
- **Maximum cumulative**: No limit (can renew multiple times)

#### Application Process & Fees

**Documents Required:**
- Passport (valid for 6+ months)
- Certificate of Eligibility (COE) — obtained from Japanese immigration office
- Visa application form (must be current version; outdated forms rejected)
- Employment contract
- University degree certificate + transcripts
- Proof of professional experience (if applicable)
- Medical examination (recent)
- Criminal background check
- All foreign-language documents require certified Japanese translations
- Passport photos (Japan-specific size/background requirements)

**Processing Timeline:**
- COE issuance: 2-3 months (after employer submits application)
- Add 1-2 months for document preparation and mailing
- Visa processing at embassy/consulate: 5-10 working days
- **Total typical timeframe: 3-5 months**

**Fees:**
- Certificate of Eligibility (COE): ¥0 (free)
- Visa issuance: ¥3,000 JPY (single-entry) / ¥6,000 JPY (multiple-entry)
- Extension of stay: ¥4,000 JPY
- Change of status: ¥4,000 JPY

#### Work Restrictions

- **Permitted**: Only work specified in employment contract at designated employer
- **Not permitted**: Self-employment, part-time work at other employers, business ownership
- **Exceptions**: Require "Permission to Engage in Activities Outside Status of Residence" (additional application)

#### Common Rejection Reasons

1. **Educational mismatch** — Degree subject doesn't align with job duties
2. **Incomplete documentation** — Missing transcripts, background check, or translations
3. **Outdated application forms** — Using old visa forms from internet
4. **Document quality issues** — Handwritten corrections, mismatched ink, overwriting
5. **Insufficient financial proof** — Unclear employment status or unstable income
6. **False or tampered documents** — Any forged credentials immediately disqualify

### 2. Intra-Company Transferee Visa

**Japanese Name**: 企業内転勤 / 企業内転勤者

**Category**: Work visa (for employees of multinational companies)

#### Requirements

**Employment History:**
- Employed by current company for **minimum 1 year** before transfer eligibility
- Must be transferring to Japanese subsidiary or branch of same company
- Permanent position (not temporary or contract)

**Salary:**
- Must earn **at least as much** as employees in same position in Japan
- Financial records demonstrating comparable compensation

**Assignment Terms:**
- Fixed duration: **3 months, 1 year, 3 years, or 5 years**
- Must have clear end date and reason for assignment
- Not open-ended or indefinite assignments

**Job Categories (must be one of):**
- Humanities/International Services (management, marketing, accounting, etc.)
- Engineering/Technical specialization

**Sponsor:**
- Japanese employer/parent company must sponsor

#### Duration & Extensions

- **Initial stay**: 3 months, 1 year, 3 years, or 5 years (fixed)
- **Extensions**: Renewable after period expires (requires reapplication)
- **Maximum cumulative**: No formal limit, but assignment feasibility reviewed each renewal

#### Application Process & Fees

**Documents Required:**
- Passport
- Certificate of Eligibility (COE)
- Employment contract (from both original and Japanese employer)
- Proof of 1+ year employment history
- Transfer assignment letter with fixed end date
- Company registration documents (both employers)
- Salary/compensation documentation
- Criminal background check
- Medical examination
- All foreign documents require Japanese translation

**Processing Timeline:**
- COE issuance: 4-6 weeks
- Total process: 2-6 months (including document prep)
- Embassy/consulate visa processing: 2-7 working days

**Fees:**
- COE: ¥0 (free)
- Visa issuance: ¥3,000 JPY (single-entry) / ¥6,000 JPY (multiple-entry)
- Extension/Change of status: ¥4,000 JPY

#### Work Restrictions

- **Permitted**: Work only for designated Japanese employer in specified role
- **Not permitted**: Work for other employers, self-employment, part-time work elsewhere
- **Exceptions**: Same as Engineer/Specialist — require separate permission

### 3. Highly Skilled Professional (HSP) Visa — Points-Based System

**Japanese Name**: 高度専門職

**Category**: Special work visa with accelerated permanent residency benefits

#### Overview

Points-based system recognizing top-tier talent. Three activity categories:

1. **Advanced Academic Research (i)(a)** — Research, teaching, guidance at institutions
2. **Advanced Specialized/Technical (i)(b)** — Technical/specialized work in natural sciences or humanities
3. **Advanced Business Management (i)(c)** — Business operation/management in Japan

#### Points System & Threshold

**Minimum Points Required: 70 points**
- Categories evaluated separately with different point criteria
- Points assigned for: academic background, professional career, annual salary, research performance, publications, patents, etc.
- **Calculator tools available** on government sites

**Income Floor:**
- **(i)(b) and (i)(c)**: Annual income must be **¥3 million minimum** or points don't count
- Even if total points ≥ 70, below ¥3M income = not recognized as HSP

#### Requirements by Category

**Advanced Academic Research (i)(a):**
- Research contract with Japanese institution
- Publication/research record
- Relevant qualifications (master's degree typically)

**Advanced Specialized/Technical (i)(b):**
- Employment in specialized/technical field
- Bachelor's degree (or equivalent experience)
- Annual income ≥ ¥3 million
- 3+ years professional experience typical
- Master's degree adds significant points

**Advanced Business Management (i)(c):**
- Business operation/management role in Japan
- Leadership experience
- Annual income ≥ ¥3 million
- Capital investment in business (context-dependent)

#### Duration & Extensions

- **Initial stay**: Up to 5 years
- **Extensions**: Renewable indefinitely
- **Path to Permanent Residency**:
  - **70-79 points**: Permanent residency eligible after **3 years** of HSP status
  - **80+ points (especially highly skilled)**: Permanent residency eligible after **1 year** of HSP status
  - Compare to regular work visa: 10 years required for permanent residency

#### Benefits

1. Accelerated permanent residency (1-3 years vs. 10 years)
2. Can include dependent family members
3. Extended initial stay period (5 years)
4. Higher priority in immigration processing
5. Multiple activity permission possible (e.g., work + manage business)

#### Application Process & Fees

**Documents Required:**
- Passport
- Certificate of Eligibility (COE)
- Employment contract
- Educational credentials (master's degree, bachelor's degree)
- Professional experience documentation
- Proof of annual income (tax returns, employment letter)
- Research publications (if applicable)
- Points calculation worksheet (completed by applicant)
- All foreign documents require Japanese translation

**Processing:**
- Similar to Engineer/Specialist visa
- COE: 2-3 months
- Total process: 3-5 months

**Fees:**
- COE: ¥0 (free)
- Visa issuance: ¥3,000 / ¥6,000 JPY
- Extension/Status change: ¥4,000 JPY

#### Work Restrictions

- **Permitted**: Work in designated category/employer as specified
- **Not permitted**: Activities outside specified scope
- **Exceptions**: Can apply for multiple activity permissions (e.g., work + business management)

### 4. Specified Skilled Worker (特定技能) — SSW1 & SSW2

**Category**: Labor visa for specific skilled trades and industries

#### Overview

Government program targeting workforce shortages in 16+ industries. Two tiers based on skill level.

**16 Authorized Sectors (as of 2024):**
1. Caregiving (介護)
2. Construction (建設)
3. Hospitality (宿泊)
4. Agriculture (農業)
5. Fisheries (漁業)
6. Food Processing (食品製造業)
7. Manufacturing (製造業)
8. Electricity and Gas (電気・ガス)
9. Semiconductor/Electronics Manufacturing (電子機器・部品製造)
10. Automobile Transportation (自動車整備) — *added 2024*
11. Railways (鉄道) — *added 2024*
12. Forestry (林業) — *added 2024*
13. Timber Industry (木材産業) — *added 2024*
14. Food Service (飲食料品製造業)
15. Cleaning (ビルクリーニング)
16. Human Resources (人材派遣業)

**NOTE**: Program continues to expand; check official website for latest updates.

#### SSW1: "Considerable Knowledge/Experience"

**Requirements:**
- **Minimum age**: 18 years old
- **Skills qualification**: One of:
  - Pass **Category 1 Specified Skills Test** (written + practical exam)
  - OR complete **3 years of Technical Intern Training (TIT) Program (ii)** (exempts from language/skills test)
- **Japanese Language**: Pass **Japanese language test** (N4 level minimum = basic conversational)
  - Exception: Exempt if completed TIT
- **Employment contract**: Full-time position with Japanese employer
- **Health/Background**: Medical exam and police clearance

**Eligible Nationalities:**
- All nationalities (most from Philippines, Vietnam, Indonesia, Thailand, Myanmar, Mongolia, Cambodia, Laos, Bangladesh)

#### SSW2: "Proficient Skills" (Supervisory Level)

**Requirements:**
- **Minimum age**: 18+ years old
- **Skills qualification**: One of:
  - Pass **Category 2 Specified Skills Test** (higher standard than SSW1)
  - OR hold **Grade 1 National Trade Skill Certificate**
  - OR have **3+ years work experience as team leader/foreman** in relevant sector
- **Japanese Language**: Pass **N3 level test** (intermediate conversational)
- **Employment contract**: Full-time position with authority to supervise/manage worksites
- **Experience**: Demonstrated supervisory/management capability

#### Duration & Extensions: Key Differences

| Aspect | SSW1 | SSW2 |
|--------|------|------|
| **Initial stay** | Up to 1 year (renewable) | Up to 3 years (renewable) |
| **Cumulative limit** | **5 years maximum** | **No limit** (indefinite renewal possible) |
| **Path to PR** | Not eligible | Eligible after 10 years |
| **Family** | No dependents allowed | May sponsor family dependents |
| **Renewal** | 1 + 4 years possible | Multiple 3-year renewals possible |

#### Application Process & Fees

**Documents Required:**
- Passport (6+ months validity)
- Certificate of Eligibility (COE)
- Category 1 or 2 skills test results
- Japanese language test results
- Medical examination
- Criminal background check
- Employment contract (clearly defining duties)
- Company registration documents
- Training completion certificate (if from TIT pathway)
- All foreign documents require Japanese translation

**Processing:**
- Skills test: Must pass before COE application
- COE issuance: 2-4 weeks (usually faster than professional visas)
- Total timeline: 6-8 weeks from test completion to visa issuance

**Fees:**
- Skills test: ¥6,000-10,000 JPY per attempt
- Japanese language test (JLPT): ¥7,700 JPY per attempt
- COE: ¥0 (free)
- Visa issuance: ¥3,000 / ¥6,000 JPY
- Extension: ¥4,000 JPY

#### Work Restrictions

- **Permitted**: Work only in assigned sector and position per contract
- **Cannot switch employers** (SSW1) without formal change of employment procedure
- **Cannot work outside sector** (e.g., construction worker cannot moonlight in hospitality)
- **No business ownership** or self-employment
- **Must notify immigration** if employment ends

#### Common Rejection Reasons (SSW-specific)

1. **Failed skills or language test** — Insufficient competency for assigned sector
2. **Employer concerns** — Immigration suspects labor trafficking or exploitation
3. **Low wages** — Salary below prevailing wage in sector
4. **Contract violations** — Terms inconsistent with sector standards or prior violations by employer
5. **False documentation** — Fake training certificates or language test results

### 5. Digital Nomad Visa — "Designated Activities"

**Japanese Name**: 特定活動（デジタルノマド）

**Category**: Special designated activities visa (launched April 2024)

#### Overview

Japan's newest visa category targeting remote workers globally. **Non-renewable** — requires reapplication after 6 months.

#### Requirements

**Work Status:**
- Remote worker for company **registered outside Japan**
- Activities: Business owner, employee, or freelancer earning income from abroad
- **NOT employed by Japanese company** (that requires work visa)

**Income Requirement:**
- **¥10 million JPY annual income minimum** (~USD $68,000-$72,000 depending on exchange rate)
- **Documentation**: Tax returns, income certificates, employment contracts, business contracts proving income
- Can be sole proprietor, freelancer, or salaried employee of foreign company
- Income must be **from outside Japan** (remote work basis)

**Insurance:**
- **Private health insurance mandatory**
- Must cover medical treatment for injury/illness up to ¥10 million (equivalent to ~USD $67,000)
- Japanese national health insurance NOT available to digital nomad visa holders
- Insurance requirements must be met before visa issuance

**Eligibility by Nationality:**
- Eligible countries: USA, Canada, most of Europe, Australia, New Zealand, Singapore, Hong Kong
- Country list subject to expansion; check official MOFA website for current list
- Does NOT include most Asian countries (China, India, Philippines, etc. typically excluded)

**Background:**
- Medical examination required
- Criminal background check (clear record)

#### Family Members

- **Dependents can be included** without additional income requirement
- Spouse and children can be added to same application
- Dependents receive same 6-month stay period

#### Duration & Renewal Rules

- **Initial stay**: **6 months (non-renewable)**
- **Reapplication**: Must exit Japan and reapply (cannot extend)
- **Gap requirement**: Must spend **6+ consecutive months outside Japan** before reapplying
- **No cumulative limit**: Can theoretically reapply multiple times with 6-month breaks

#### Application Process & Fees

**Documents Required:**
- Passport (valid 6+ months)
- Application form (designated activities visa form)
- Certificate of Eligibility (COE) application or direct visa application (varies by country)
- Proof of annual income (¥10M+):
  - Last 2 years tax returns or income statements
  - Employment contract from foreign employer
  - Business contract/engagement letters (if freelance)
  - Bank statements showing regular transfers
- Private health insurance documentation:
  - Policy coverage up to ¥10 million
  - Premium payment proof
- Medical examination results (recent)
- Criminal background check
- Travel history (last 3-5 years)
- Accommodation information in Japan (address, proof of residence)
- All foreign documents require Japanese translation

**Processing:**
- Timing varies by Japanese embassy/consulate
- Some allow online application (eVISA as of Dec 2025 for select countries)
- Estimated 2-4 weeks from completed application submission

**Fees:**
- Visa application fee: ¥0 (typically free for designated activities)
- Visa issuance: ¥3,000 JPY (single-entry) — may vary
- Some embassies may waive fees (confirm with local consulate)

#### Work Restrictions & Permissions

**Permitted:**
- Remote work for foreign company/clients
- Business ownership (if company registered abroad)
- Freelance/consulting for international clients
- Teaching online (for foreign institutions)
- Writing, content creation (for foreign publishers)

**NOT Permitted:**
- Employment by Japanese company or organization
- On-site work at Japanese office (even for same company)
- Business activities involving Japanese clients or contracts registered in Japan
- Any income-generating activity in Japan
- Dependent work without separate visa status

**Important**: Digital nomad visa is **not a work permit in Japan** — it's a residency visa. Remote work must be entirely for foreign entities.

#### Visa Categories on Digital Nomad Visa

Three related statuses under "Designated Activities":
1. **Digital Nomad** (primary visa holder)
2. **Spouse of Digital Nomad** (accompanying family member)
3. **Child of Digital Nomad** (accompanying family member)

### 6. Long-Term Resident Visa (特定活動 — Long-term Resident)

**Category**: Special designated activities visa (humanitarian/family-based)

#### Overview

Granted at **discretion of Immigration Services Agency** based on individual circumstances. Not formulaic like other visas — each case evaluated uniquely.

**Purpose**: Allow foreign nationals with strong ties to Japan (family, residency history) to continue living in Japan when standard visa categories don't fit.

#### Common Eligibility Scenarios

1. **Divorced/Widowed from Japanese spouse** — Raising dependent children with Japanese custody
2. **Parent of Japanese child** — Sole custodian of child born in Japan
3. **Bringing stepchildren to Japan** — Establishing blended family in Japan
4. **Japanese descendant (Nikkeijin)** — Foreign-born person with Japanese heritage
5. **Long-term residents** — Individuals with 10+ years residency history, established community ties, stable employment
6. **Humanitarian cases** — Medical, family, or other exceptional circumstances

#### Requirements (General)

- **Previous residency in Japan**: Typically several years (varies by case)
- **Financial stability**: Demonstrated ability to support self (and dependents if applicable)
- **Law-abiding record**: No criminal history, no visa violations, tax/insurance payments current
- **Community ties**: Employment, family connections, housing stability
- **Change in circumstances**: Clear reason why previous visa status is no longer suitable
- **Japanese language**: Not required, but basic ability helpful for integration

#### Duration & Renewal

- **Initial stay**: Discretionary (typically 1-3 years)
- **Extensions**: Must reapply at expiration
- **Reconsideration each renewal**: Circumstances reviewed every period
- **Cumulative**: No theoretical limit if renewed indefinitely

#### Work Permissions

- **Very permissive**: Allowed to work in most industries
- **Exceptions**: Regulated professions (law, medicine, etc.) have separate licensing
- **Business ownership**: Permitted (unlike some work visas)
- **No restrictions on employer switching**

#### Important Limitations

- **Not guaranteed renewal**: Circumstances examined at each expiration
  - Divorce from Japanese spouse = likely denied
  - Custody changes = may affect eligibility
  - Job loss without replacement = problematic
  - Reduced income = scrutinized
- **Discretionary nature**: No objective scoring; decisions can be unpredictable
- **Background checks**: Immigration verifies tax payments, insurance premiums, employment stability
- **Even paid-back debts matter**: Unpaid taxes/insurance in past = negative factor even if paid now (as of Feb 2026 rule change)

#### Application Process & Fees

**Documents Required:**
- Passport
- Current residence card
- Application form (特定活動申請書)
- Proof of residency (utility bills, lease)
- Employment contract/business registration (if applicable)
- Proof of financial stability (tax returns, bank statements, employment letter)
- Marriage certificate (original or divorce decree if applicable)
- Birth certificates of dependent children
- Custody documentation (if applicable)
- Medical examination
- Criminal background check
- All foreign documents require Japanese translation

**Processing:**
- Immigration office reviews: 1-3 months
- Interviewed at immigration office: Yes, typically (individual case assessment)
- Notification: By mail when decision made

**Fees:**
- Application: ¥0-4,000 JPY (varies; extension typically ¥4,000)
- Visa issuance: ¥0 (no visa stamp; issued as status change)

### 7. Instructor Visa (Teaching/Education)

**Japanese Name**: 教育

**Category**: Work visa (for educators in schools/institutions)

#### Requirements

**Education:**
- Bachelor's degree required (in any field)
- Teaching qualification/license (varies by position)
  - Public schools: Often require Japanese teaching license or JLPT N1
  - Private schools/language academies: Bachelor's degree often sufficient

**Position:**
- Full-time teaching position at Japanese educational institution
- School/institution must sponsor visa application
- Employment contract clearly defining teaching duties

**Language:**
- JLPT not strictly required for work visa approval
- However, practical teaching typically requires:
  - N2-N1 for classroom instruction in Japanese
  - English-only schools waive this
  - Language academies may accept lower levels (especially for English-specific training)

**Salary:**
- Must be competitive with Japanese teachers in equivalent position
- No specific minimum, but must align with job market

#### Duration & Extensions

- **Initial stay**: 1, 3, or 5 years
- **Extensions**: Renewable indefinitely
- **Cumulative**: No limit

#### Application Process & Fees

**Documents Required:**
- Passport
- Certificate of Eligibility (COE)
- Employment contract
- University degree certificate
- Teaching credentials (if applicable)
- Medical examination
- Criminal background check (critical for education sector)
- If teaching English: TEFL/TESOL certificate optional but helpful
- All foreign documents require Japanese translation

**Processing:**
- COE: 2-3 months
- Total: 3-5 months

**Fees:**
- Same as Engineer/Specialist visa
- COE: ¥0
- Visa: ¥3,000-¥6,000 JPY
- Extension: ¥4,000 JPY

#### Work Restrictions

- **Permitted**: Only teaching at designated institution
- **Not permitted**: Part-time tutoring, other employment, tutoring without school sponsorship (unless under separate permission)
- **Can apply for**: Multiple activity permission to allow supplementary education work

## Part 5: Recent Policy Changes (2024-2026)

### A. Business Manager (Investor) Visa — Significantly Stricter

**Starting October 2025:**

| Requirement | Old (pre-Oct 2025) | New (Oct 2025+) |
|-------------|------|-------|
| Capital investment | ¥5 million | **¥30 million** (6× increase) |
| Employees required | 2 full-time | **1 full-time** (slightly relaxed) |
| Japanese language | Not mandatory | **B2 (JLPT N2)** now mandatory |
| Duration | 1, 3, 5 years | Same (1, 3, 5 years) |

**Impact**: Fewer foreign entrepreneurs expected to qualify; existing Business Manager visa holders unaffected if renewed before Oct 2025.

### B. Permanent Residency — New Stricter Rules (Feb 2026)

**Major change effective February 24, 2026:**

- **Mandatory 5-year hold**: Applicants must hold the **longest available visa period under current status** (typically 5 years) before permanent residency eligibility
  - Previously: Could apply after shorter cumulative periods
  - Now: 5-year Engineer/Specialist or equivalent required
- **Tax/Insurance screening tightened**: Immigration now scrutinizes past **unpaid taxes and health insurance premiums**
  - Even if paid off since, negative history flags applications
  - Perfect payment history now nearly mandatory
- **Age considerations**: Older applicants (50+) scrutinized more closely
- **Income stability**: Multiple job changes viewed negatively

**Implication**: Path to permanent residency now significantly longer (5+ years minimum holding required visa status).

### C. Digital Nomad Visa Launch (April 2024)

- **Status**: New "Designated Activities" visa for remote workers
- **Duration**: 6 months, non-renewable
- **Income requirement**: ¥10 million annually
- **Private insurance**: Mandatory (¥10 million coverage)
- **Eligible countries**: USA, Canada, Australia, NZ, most of Europe, Singapore, Hong Kong (expanding)

### D. Fee Increases (FY2026)

- **2025 baseline increases**: 5-10% across visa types
- **FY2026 planned increases**: 5-10× for major categories
  - Visa change fee: Expected ~¥40,000 (from current ¥4,000)
  - Permanent residency fee: Expected ~¥100,000 (from current ¥0-4,000)

### E. eVISA Expansion (December 2025)

- **Countries now eligible** for online application:
  - USA, Canada, UK, Australia, New Zealand, South Africa, Brazil, Saudi Arabia, Taiwan, Cambodia
- **Benefits**: Reduced in-person embassy visits, faster processing
- **Rollout**: Expanding to additional countries ongoing

### F. International Graduate Benefit (December 1, 2025)

- **New policy**: Graduates from Japanese universities/vocational schools can apply for Engineer/Specialist visa with **reduced documentation requirements**
- **Benefit**: Smoother transition from Student to work visa status
- **Eligibility**: Must have completed 2+ years of study in Japan

### G. Employment for Skill Development (2027 Implementation)

- **Replaces**: Technical Intern Training Program (being phased out)
- **Structure**: Clear training-to-employment pathway
- **Benefits**: Clearer regulatory framework, fewer labor exploitation concerns
- **Timeframe**: Transition period 2025-2027; TIT and ESD may coexist temporarily

### H. Foreign Worker Cap Expansion

- **Government target**: 1.23 million foreign workers (announced 2025)
- **Rationale**: Address Japan's demographic crisis and labor shortages
- **Timeline**: Gradual expansion through 2027-2030
- **Focus sectors**: Caregiving, construction, hospitality, agriculture, manufacturing

## Part 7: Common Pain Points & Application Challenges

### Documentation Issues (Most Frequent)

1. **Outdated visa forms** — Embassies immediately reject old forms; always download from official MOFA website
2. **Missing translations** — Every foreign-language document requires certified Japanese translation
3. **Inconsistent information** — Name spellings, dates, or details must match exactly across all documents
4. **Insufficient financial proof**:
   - Bank statements with low balance → rejection
   - Irregular deposits flagged
   - Need 6+ months of statements showing stability
   - Employment letter should detail salary/employment terms clearly
5. **Photo specifications not met**:
   - Strict size requirements (4×3cm typically)
   - White background only
   - No filters, shadows, or hats
   - Recent photo only (within 6 months)

### Visa Category Mismatches

- **Applying for tourist visa when traveling for work** → Likely rejection
- **Engineering student trying to work on student visa without permission** → Violation, deportation risk
- **SSW worker attempting to change employers** → Requires formal change of employment procedure; unauthorized work = violation

### Educational/Experience Documentation Problems

- **Bachelor's degree from non-recognized institution** → Scrutinized; may require evaluation service
- **Certificate of Eligibility (COE) rejection** — Often due to:
  - Degree field doesn't match job duties
  - Employer questionable (new startup, financial instability)
  - Job description too vague
  - Too many job changes in short time

### Financial Red Flags

- **Sudden large deposits** before application → Immigration suspects false proof
- **Accounts with minimal activity** → Suggests financial instability
- **Salary inconsistent with job title** → Employer credibility questioned
- **Job loss near application date** → Creates uncertainty about future sponsorship

### Background Check Issues

- **Previous visa overstays** (any country) → Major concern; scrutinized heavily
- **Criminal records** → Disqualifying in most cases
- **Deportation history** → Extremely difficult to overcome
- **Unpaid taxes/insurance in Japan** → Now negative factor even if paid (2026 rule)

### Immigration Violations

- **Working without proper visa** (e.g., student working without permission) → Deportable
- **Employer-sponsored visa but working for different employer** → Violation
- **Overstaying permit** (even by 1 day) → Criminal record, future visa denial

### Common Applicant Mistakes

1. **Rushing document preparation** → Errors, omissions, typos cause rejection
2. **Not verifying current form versions** → Downloaded old form from Google search
3. **Poor translation quality** → Using non-professional translator or machine translation
4. **Submitting tampered documents** — Handwritten corrections, whiteout, mismatched fonts → immediate rejection/fraud suspicion
5. **Incomplete COE application** by employer — Missing company seal, incomplete sections
6. **Lying about employment** or fabricating job offers → Creates fraud record if discovered
7. **Underestimating processing times** — Applying too close to travel date; visa denied due to insufficient processing time

### Reapplication Rules

- **If visa is rejected**: Cannot reapply for **same visa type within 6 months**
- **No feedback given**: Immigration does not disclose rejection reasons (Administrative Procedure Act)
- **Must wait 6 months** before next attempt (full reboot needed if similar reason)

### Digital Nomad-Specific Pain Points

- **Income documentation often rejected** if:
  - Inconsistent across 2-year history
  - Recent income spike looks suspicious
  - Invoice-based income hard to verify
- **Insurance coverage verification** — Must confirm policy meets ¥10M requirement
- **Reapplication fatigue** — 6-month duration + 6-month gap required = 1-year commitment to stay/reapply cycle

- **Temporary Visitor**: No work allowed

- **Specified Skilled Worker (SSW)**: Requires formal "Change of Employment" procedure; cannot simply switch employers
- **Work visas generally**: Authorized employment limited to specified employer (with exceptions for HSP, LTR)

## Appendix: Key Definitions & Terms

**Certificate of Eligibility (COE)** — Document from Japanese immigration office confirming visa applicant is eligible for status of residence. Required before overseas visa issuance at embassy/consulate.

**Status of Residence (在留資格)** — Legal category defining what activities a foreigner may perform and length of stay in Japan. Examples: Engineer/Specialist, Student, Permanent Resident, etc.

**Change of Status of Residence** — Application to switch visa categories while already in Japan (e.g., from Student to Engineer). Fee: ¥4,000 JPY.

**Permission to Engage in Activities Outside Status of Residence** — Separate permission allowing work outside primary visa scope (e.g., student working part-time). Application fee varies.

**Residence Card (在留カード)** — Physical ID card carried by all non-Japanese residents, issued by immigration upon visa approval. Must be renewed annually or when status changes.

**Overstay (オーバーステイ)** — Remaining in Japan beyond visa expiration. Illegal, results in deportation and future visa denial.

**JLPT (日本語能力試験)** — Japanese Language Proficiency Test. Levels N5-N1 (N1 = highest proficiency). N4 = basic, N3 = intermediate, N2 = advanced.

**Highly Skilled Professional (HSP) Status** — Special visa category with accelerated permanent residency path based on points (70+ = HSP; 80+ = especially highly skilled).

**Technical Intern Training (TIT)** — Labor program bringing foreign trainees to Japan for 1-3 years to learn skills. Being phased out and replaced by ESD (Employment for Skill Development) by 2027.
