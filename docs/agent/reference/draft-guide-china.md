## TL;DR Card

**Budget Range (Monthly, All-In)**
- Budget tier: $1,000–1,300 (Dali, Chengdu)
- Comfortable tier: $1,500–2,000 (Shanghai, Beijing, Shenzhen)
- Splurge tier: $2,500+ (luxury Shanghai/Beijing lifestyle)

**Visa Options**
- **No official digital nomad visa exists.** China requires work permits (Z-visa) for employment. Remote workers typically operate in a legal gray area on tourist/business visas.

| Visa Type | Duration | Purpose | Best For |
|-----------|----------|---------|----------|
| **Z-Visa (Work)** | 1 year | Official work visa; requires employer sponsorship | Highest legitimacy; for employed remote workers |

**Internet Reality**
The Great Firewall (China's internet censorship and filtering system) blocks: Google, WhatsApp, Instagram, Telegram, and most Western apps. VPN essential to access these (tolerated for foreigners, legally gray). Internet speed: 4G/5G excellent for Chinese content; degraded for international traffic via VPN.

**Safety**
Very safe. Heavy surveillance deters violent crime. Petty theft rare in major cities. No war/terrorism risk.

**English Friendliness**
⭐⭐ (2/5) — Shanghai & Beijing: limited to business/tourist areas. Outside tier-1 cities: Mandarin essential. Assume English unavailable and plan accordingly.

**Best Months**
- **Best:** March–May (spring), September–October (autumn) — mild temps, lowest pollution
- **Avoid:** October–February (northern pollution), June–August (hot, humid, rainy), Golden Week (Oct 1–7: overcrowded)

## The China Reality Check

Let's be direct: China is **not** an easy digital nomad destination. Here's why:

- **No legal path to stay long-term remotely.** You cannot legally work for a foreign company on a tourist visa. The gray area exists, but it carries actual risk.
- **The Great Firewall is relentless.** Every day, you'll need a VPN to access Western tools. VPN protocols get blocked, then updated. It's constant friction.
- **Language matters.** Shanghai has enough English to navigate. Outside tier-1 cities, survival requires Mandarin or serious app skills.
- **WeChat is non-negotiable.** Your payment method, ID verification, messaging platform, work tool, and social network: all WeChat.

**Why come anyway?**
- Lowest costs in Asia (outside SEA)
- Astonishing infrastructure (metro, rail, delivery)
- Massive expat community in Shanghai (500K+)
- Growth mindset if you're interested in China seriously
- Dali offers lifestyle paradise at $1,000/month
- Scale of ambition (mega-cities attract builders)

## Visa Options: The Gray Area Explained

### The Brutal Truth
China published requirements for Z, X, L, M visas — but **no official "digital nomad visa" exists**. Remote workers occupy a legal gray zone.

**Published Guidelines Indicate:**
- **Z-Visa (Work)** requires a Chinese employer or foreign company's China subsidiary to sponsor you. Self-employed/freelance remote work: not officially permitted.
- **Tourist Visa (L)** is for leisure; employment prohibited.
- **Business Visa (M)** is for conference/meetings; remote work: unclear.

**Reality on Ground (2025–2026):**
- Many digital nomads operate on L or M visas. Enforcement is **selective**: focus is on Chinese employers hiring illegally, not tourists/remote workers.
- Risk profile: Low if you're invisible (no employer presence, no local income). Higher if you draw attention (e.g., teaching/content creation for Chinese audience).

**New: K-Visa for Global Talent (October 2025)** [2] (K-Visa = China's new high-skilled talent visa, distinct from the work visa Z-Visa)
- Introduced for high-skilled professionals in STEM fields with bachelor's degree or equivalent
- Requires proof of medical insurance (specific amount to be confirmed by embassy)
- Advantages over Z-Visa: More lenient residence permit rules; no employer sponsorship required
- Not yet widely used; still emerging but likely to become more common for international tech workers

## First 72 Hours Checklist: Critical Setup

China requires more upfront friction than most countries. Here's the essential sequence:

### Before Landing (Critical)
- [ ] Install & activate VPN before boarding. Once in China, download speeds for new VPN clients are crippled. Test on departure airport wifi.
- [ ] Activate eSIM with international carrier (Jetpac, SimOptions, Holafly). QR codes won't load inside firewall; activate online, bring QR confirmation code.
- [ ] Download offline maps: Google Maps (offline), Citymaps2Go, or Amap (Chinese maps app, English available)
- [ ] Download WeChat on your phone; account creation requires phone verification (works with international number)

### Airport Arrival (Hour 0–2)
- [ ] Exit immigration; connect to airport VPN/eSIM immediately
- [ ] Purchase backup SIM if desired (tourist SIM: ~¥100, but slower than eSIM)
- [ ] Exchange small cash (¥500–1,000) for taxi, food (some vendors still cash-only)
- [ ] Test WeChat Pay/Alipay setup at convenience store

### Day 1: PSB Registration (Critical Legal Requirement)
- [ ] **Register with Public Security Bureau (PSB) within 24 hours.** This is non-optional. PSB is China's national police authority that manages foreigner registration. Violation: ¥2,000 fine (~$275) [8] + complications for visa extension.
  - Required documents: Passport, visa, accommodation proof (landlord letter, hotel receipt, university enrollment)
  - Location: Local PSB office (in some cities, accessible through "WeChat → Services → PSB Registration" mini-program to skip the line)
  - Time: 15–30 minutes
- [ ] Set up Chinese mobile number (link eSIM to account or buy tourist SIM)
- [ ] Create WeChat account; link international payment card or use 90-day tour pass

### Day 1–2: Digital Setup
- [ ] **WeChat Pay setup:** (Tencent's dominant payment app; 1+ billion users; used everywhere)
  - Download Weixin app (Chinese name for WeChat)
  - Link international credit card (Visa/MasterCard/Amex)
  - Transaction limits: ¥6,000/transaction, ¥50,000/month without bank account
- [ ] **Alipay setup:** (Alibaba's payment system; main competitor to WeChat Pay; ~30% market share)
  - Download Alipay app
  - Link international card
  - Claim 90-day tour pass (no bank account needed)
  - Transaction limits: ¥3,000/transaction, ¥50,000/month
  - **Choice:** WeChat dominates but Alipay is a useful backup; most stores accept both
- [ ] Open bank account (optional, unlocks higher transaction limits): ICBC, Bank of China, ABC accept foreigners with Z/X/M visa + proof of residence
- [ ] Download delivery apps: Meituan (food delivery, 60% market share), Ele.me (Alibaba's delivery platform, 30% market share) — 90% combined market dominance
- [ ] Test VPN reliability; have backup VPN installed

### Day 2–3: Neighborhood Setup
- [ ] Find coworking space or cafe with good wifi (test VPN performance)
- [ ] Join local WeChat groups: search "Shanghai digital nomad," "Beijing expats," etc.
- [ ] Locate grocery stores (Freshippo/Hema, Sam's Club, local markets)
- [ ] Discover 2–3 go-to restaurants within walking distance
- [ ] Set up DiDi (ride-hailing) account; accept English-language app

### 6-Month+ Plan (Legitimacy Threshold)

**Critical 183-Day Tax Rule** (China's tax residency threshold)
- Staying 183+ days in any 12-month period = "tax resident" status in China
- Tax resident = liable for China-source income tax on your salary (3–45% progressive tax brackets). This only applies to income earned in China.
- **6-year rule:** After 6 consecutive years at 183+ days/year, worldwide income becomes taxable from year 7 (even remote work for foreign clients becomes taxable)
- **Reset mechanism:** 30+ consecutive days outside China in any year resets the counter to zero

**Visa Strategy for 6+ Months**
- **Z-Visa (Work):** 1-year validity. Requires employer sponsorship (Chinese company or foreign company's subsidiary). Most legitimate, but requires actual job contract.
- **Student visa (X-Visa):** Enroll in language school or university; 6–12 month durations common; ~$1,500–2,500 program fees
- **R-Visa (Talent):** For skilled professionals; emerging option with better terms
- **Cumulative tourist visas:** Keep leaving/re-entering to stay under 183 days. Complex but used by some

**Health Insurance Requirement (NEW 2025)**
- K-visa: Requires ¥100,000+ coverage (mandatory)
- Z-visa: Requires ¥100,000+ coverage (mandatory)
- Private insurance: $4,500–10,000+/year depending on coverage
- Employers typically provide (included in Z-visa packages)

**Tax Implications**
- China-source income: taxed at 3–45% depending on bracket
- Remote work for foreign employer: legally gray; technically taxable as China-source income
- **Recommendation:** Hire expat tax accountant familiar with China + your home country. Cost: $500–1,500/year, worthwhile to avoid penalties.

**Long-Term Accommodation**
- Sign 12-month apartment lease: $5,000–8,000/month rent but can negotiate to $3,500–5,500 for committed term
- Expat compounds: $1,500–3,000/month for furnished, all-in

## Housing: Finding, Negotiating, Avoiding Scams

This applies to all cities. Read before apartment hunting.

### Where to Find Apartments

**Lianjia (链家) — Largest Platform**
- **What:** Real estate aggregator; connects individual owners + agencies
- **How to use:**
  1. Filter by neighborhood, price, size
  2. Contact agents directly (many speak English in tier-1 cities)
  3. Schedule viewings (same day often possible)
- **Advantage:** Transparent pricing, large inventory, professional agents
- **Caveat:** Agency fees typically 1 month's rent
- **Hidden gem:** More reliable than private landlord listings (less scam risk)

**Ziroom (自如) — For Furnished Rentals**
- **What:** Specialist in furnished apartments ("move-in ready")
- **Benefits:**
  - Fully furnished (bed, couch, desk, etc.)
  - Utilities often included
  - Professional cleaning, maintenance included
  - **Hidden gem:** English mobile app (easier for non-Chinese speakers)
- **Cost:** 10–20% higher than unfurnished
- **Best for:** Short-term nomads (3–6 months), minimal packing
- **How to use:** English app is straightforward; similar to Airbnb

**WeChat Housing Groups — Direct Landlords**
- **What:** Local groups (hyperlocal, neighborhood-based) where landlords post listings
- **How to find:** Ask other expats in your target city
- **Advantage:** Direct landlord contact, no agency fees
- **Disadvantage:** Less vetting; scams more common
- **Hidden gem:** Active groups often have listings 24–48 hours before Lianjia

### Negotiation Strategy (Landlords Expect It)

**Reality:** Prices are often set high; negotiation is normal and expected

**Typical outcomes:**
- 5–6% off asking price is normal
- 10% off with agent help (agent incentivized to close)
- More with long lease commitment (6–12 months) = 10–15% off
- 6+ months upfront payment = additional 5% discount

**Negotiation tactics:**
- Long lease (6–12 months): "I'm staying long-term; more discount?"
- Pay upfront (6 months): "I'll pay 6 months now; give me discount?"
- Bundle (multiple apartments): "I know other expats; if you give discount, I bring them"
- Perks instead of price: "Price is ¥X, but can we get free WiFi + new furniture + AC unit?"

**Language workaround:**
- Landlords rarely speak English
- **Solution:** WeChat's built-in translation (works well for written contracts)
- Or hire Chinese friend to negotiate (worth it; saves thousands)

### Common Scams to Avoid

**Red flags (Skip these):**
- Landlord unwilling to sign contract
- Payment to personal account (not escrow)
- Pressure to decide same day
- No receipt for deposit
- Landlord vague about when you can move in
- "Too good to be true" pricing (¥3,000 for luxury apartment in Shanghai = scam)

**Safe process:**
1. **Contract (written):** Get English translation; don't sign without reading
2. **Escrow:** Many agents provide third-party escrow (money held until move-in)
3. **Receipts:** Get for every payment
4. **48-hour walkaway:** Confirm ability to back out within 48 hours if apartment condition differs
5. **Deposit terms:** Contract must clearly state you get deposit back if no damage

### Deposit Strategy (Sesame Credit)

**New option:** Link Alipay Sesame Credit to avoid deposits

- **Sesame Credit:** Alipay's credit rating system
- **Benefit:** Build reputation; use to waive deposits
- **How:** Pay consistently and on-time to boost score
- **Long-term gain:** Future landlords see good score; may waive deposit entirely
- **Also works for:** Bike-sharing, utility deposits, rental agreements

## Taxes & Legal: The Compliance Reality

### The 183-Day Tax Residency Rule

**Core Rule** [12]
- Fewer than 183 days in China per calendar year = NOT tax resident (China-source income only)
- 183+ days = "tax resident"; liable for China-source income tax (3–45% progressive brackets)
- Count method: Each full 24-hour calendar day = 1 day (not work days)

**Practical Example**
- Jan 15: Arrive in China (day 1)
- June 15: Depart (day 152)
- Not tax resident; not liable for China income tax
- But if you're working remotely for Chinese client, that income is taxable

---

### The Six-Year Rule (CRITICAL FOR 2025)

**The Trap** [12]
- 183+ days/year for 6 **consecutive** years = worldwide income becomes taxable from Year 7 onward

**Impact**
- Years 1–6: Only China-source income taxable (e.g., salary from Chinese employer)
- Year 7 onward: ALL income taxable (e.g., remote work for foreign company)

**Reset Mechanism**
- 30+ consecutive days outside China in any year = counter resets
- Example: Spend July 1–31 outside China; counter resets; count starts fresh

**2025 Critical Threshold** [12]
Expatriates who arrived in 2019 and maintained 183+ days/year for 6 years just hit the worldwide income threshold in 2025. This is a **critical year** for tax planning.

---

### Individual Income Tax (IIT) Rates
(IIT = China's personal income tax, applied monthly to salaries and self-employment income)

**Tax Brackets (Simplified)**
- 0–1,500 CNY: 3%
- 1,500–4,500 CNY: 10%
- 4,500–9,000 CNY: 20%
- 9,000–35,000 CNY: 25%
- 35,000–55,000 CNY: 30%
- 55,000–80,000 CNY: 35%
- 80,000+ CNY: 45%

(Applied monthly; progressive tax calculation)

**What's Taxable**
- Salary/wages from Chinese employer
- Self-employment income (consulting, freelance)
- Rental income from China property
- Investment income (stocks, bonds)
- Remote work income (gray area; technically taxable as China-source)

**Foreign Tax Credit**
- China offers credit for taxes paid to other countries
- Prevents double taxation
- Requires documentation from home country tax authority

---

### Filing Requirements & Penalties

**Who Must File**
- Tax residents earning China-source income
- Self-employed/freelance workers
- Anyone with employment income

**Deadline**
- Annual filing: By March 31 following tax year
- Can backdate filings, but penalties accrue (interest + fines)

**Penalties (If Non-Compliant)**
- Late filing: 0.5% monthly interest + 5% penalty
- Tax evasion: Up to 5× unpaid tax + criminal liability (rare for honest mistakes)

**Professional Help**
- Hire expat tax accountant: $500–1,500/year
- Worthwhile for any serious income
- Accountant handles filing; you provide docs

---

### Remote Work Legal Gray Area

**The Truth**
- Working remotely for foreign employer on tourist/business visa: Technically illegal
- Enforcement: Extremely low for tourists/short-term residents; higher for long-term with visible employer presence
- Risk assessment:
  - **Low risk:** Freelance, personal projects, no employer in China
  - **Medium risk:** Remote employee for foreign company (tax implications worst-case; visa implications low)
  - **High risk:** Working for Chinese employer without Z-visa (active enforcement)

**Tax Implication**
- Remote work income is China-source income (even if for foreign company)
- Subject to China income tax if you're tax resident (183+ days)
- Must declare and file

**Visa Implication**
- Working without work permit: Technically violation
- Enforcement: Low for invisible workers; high for those with Chinese employer/visible operations
- Worst-case: Fined, deported, banned

**Honest Recommendation**
- You're operating in gray area; accept the risk
- Or: Get Z-visa (requires employer sponsorship; only viable if working for China employer or foreign company willing to establish subsidiary)
- Or: Stay under 183 days (avoid tax residency; harder for longer stays)
- Consult immigration lawyer if serious about staying 6+ months

### PSB Registration: The 24-Hour Rule

**Non-Negotiable Rule** [8]
All foreigners must register temporary residence with Public Security Bureau within 24 hours of arrival in China.

**Applies To**
- Anyone NOT staying in a hotel or university dorm
- Hotels/dorms handle registration automatically; you're exempt

**What Happens (Timeline)**
1. **Arrive in China**
2. **Check into accommodation** (Airbnb, apartment, hostel, etc.)
3. **Within 24 hours:** Go to local PSB office with:
   - Passport
   - Visa page
   - Accommodation proof (landlord letter, apartment lease, booking confirmation)
   - Filled form (PSB provides)
4. **Officer registers** temporary residence (15–30 minutes)
5. **Receive confirmation** (usually in passport)

**Penalties for Non-Compliance** [8]
- Minor violation: Warning
- Financial: ¥2,000 fine ($274)
- Serious: Cannot apply for visa extension, residence permit, police certificate
- Exit issues: May be flagged; could be denied exit

**2025–2026 Status**
- Enforcement: Active and consistent across all cities
- Digitization: Some cities now have WeChat mini-program registration (avoid long lines)
- Mobile police: Tourist areas have mobile units for travelers

**Nomad Shortcuts**

**Option 1: Hotel Registration (Easiest)**
- Stay at hotel first night (they register automatically)
- Move to apartment day 2
- Hotel registration is valid if you keep receipt

**Option 2: Landlord/Agent Handles It**
- Many landlords/agents will register for you (common practice)
- Fee: Usually free or ¥50–100
- **Pro tip:** Ask when signing lease: "Can you register at PSB for me?"
- Saves 1–2 hours and the stress of navigating PSB

**Option 3: Digital Registration (Some Cities)**
- Beijing, Shanghai: WeChat mini-program registration available
- Search: "WeChat → Services → PSB Registration" or similar
- Avoids long lines; can do from apartment

**Option 4: Mobile Police (Tourist Areas)**
- Major tourist areas often have mobile registration units
- Airports, train stations have temporary units
- Avoids PSB office visit

## Culture & Daily Life: The Unwritten Rules

### Guanxi & Face

**Guanxi (关系 Relationships)** — This is the concept of personal connections and social capital in China
- Business relationships built on personal trust and informal connections, not just contracts
- Investment in relationships pays long-term dividends (clients stay longer, deals are better)
- Informal dinners/drinks crucial for bonding (relationship-building is an investment, not just socializing)
- Guan = gate/entrance; xi = connection (literally: who you know matters more than what you know)

**Face (面子 Reputation/Dignity)** — The concept of public respect and personal honor
- Direct criticism causes loss of face (highly avoided in professional settings)
- Saying "no" directly is considered rude; use indirect refusal instead ("I'll think about it" / "Maybe next time")
- Public recognition important (being praised in front of peers is highly valued)
- Avoid correcting someone in front of others (especially in business contexts)
- Humility valued (don't boast about achievements; let others praise you)

**Practical Implications**
- Negotiations take time (relationship-building phase)
- Decisions may take longer (consensus-seeking)
- Informal agreements sometimes more binding than contracts
- Personal trust > written rules

**QR Codes: Everything is a QR Code**
- **Reality:** Nearly every daily transaction requires scanning a QR code
- **What's scanned:**
  - Restaurant payment (after eating, scan QR to pay)
  - Bike sharing unlock (Meituan Bike, Hello Bike)
  - WiFi login (especially hotels, cafes)
  - Taxi payment (after ride, driver shows QR)
  - Metro card recharge (some cities)
  - Government services (forms, permits)
  - Store entrance (temperature check during COVID; still sometimes used)
- **Nomad survival:** Learn to spot and scan QR codes; this is how modern China works
- **App:** Google Lens (if VPN on) or WeChat's built-in QR scanner (works offline)
- **No cash needed:** Most places accept WeChat/Alipay payment via QR
- **Pro tip:** If you see a QR code at a shop/restaurant and unsure what it does, just scan (merchants expect this)

### Red Envelope Culture (Hongbao) (红包 Red Packet — Cash gift tradition)

**When You'll Encounter It**
- Chinese New Year (biggest occasion; offices, family, friends exchange red envelopes with cash inside)
- Weddings, births, promotions (smaller envelopes to celebrate the occasion)
- Business partnerships or starting new business relationships (small gift to formalize relationship)

**Amount Etiquette**
- Even numbers lucky (2, 8, 10, 100)
- **Avoid:** 4 (sounds like death in Chinese); 7 (sometimes unlucky)
- **Typical amounts:**
  - Close friend/family: ¥200–500
  - Colleague: ¥100–200
  - Boss: ¥500–1,000
  - New business partner: ¥100–300 (formalize relationship)
- **Social rule:** Open later, alone (opening in front = rude; implies checking amount)
- **Refusing:** Polite refusal → accept anyway (expected dance; eventually accept)
- **Giving back:** If unsure, can give digital hongbao instead of cash

**Digital Hongbao (WeChat & Alipay)**
- **WeChat hongbao:** Send virtual red envelope through app (¥1–500)

- **Cultural note:** Digital hongbao maintain tradition while solving cash scarcity
- **Pro tip:** If no cash on hand, WeChat hongbao acceptable (actually preferred by many)

- Younger people more likely to speak some English
- Older generation: Almost no English

**Survival Strategy**
- Download Google Translate (offline mode); point camera at text
- Pleco app: Dictionary with OCR (photo Chinese characters, get definition)
- Speak slowly; simple sentences
- Have translation card written out for key phrases
- Use illustration-heavy menus (point at pictures)

## Weather & Best Times

### Seasonal Air Quality (AQI Data 2025)

**Winter (Oct–Feb): Worst for North**
- Average AQI: 89 (poor quality)
- Heating season increases coal/biomass pollution
- Temperature inversions trap smog
- January, December: Worst months
- **Avoid:** Beijing and northern China
- **Okay:** Southern cities (Hainan, Yunnan, Guangxi; AQI 40–60)

**Summer (June–Aug): Best Air Quality**
- Average AQI: 67 (moderate; best of year)
- Lowest pollution overall
- Hot and humid in most regions
- High precipitation (rainy season)
- Mosquitoes more active

**Spring (Mar–May): Good Overall**
- Mild temperatures
- Lower pollution (except dust storms in north, Mar–Apr)
- Comfortable humidity
- Peak tourist season (crowded)

**Autumn (Sep–Oct): Ideal**
- Excellent air quality
- Mild temperatures (15–25°C / 59–77°F)
- Lower humidity
- **Best time to visit**

### Regional Variations

**Northern China (Beijing, Xi'an, Shanxi)**
- Winter: Severe pollution (smog), cold (−5 to 5°C / 23–41°F)
- Spring: Dust storms possible (March–April)
- Summer: Hot (30–35°C / 86–95°F); lower pollution
- Autumn: Best season overall
- **Avoid:** October–February if pollution-sensitive

**Southern China (Shanghai, Guangzhou, Chengdu)**
- Winter: Mild but smoggy (5–15°C / 41–59°F)
- Spring: Good, mild (15–25°C)
- Summer: Hot, humid (30–38°C / 86–100°F); rainy season
- Autumn: Excellent
- Overall: More stable than north

**Yunnan (Dali, Lijiang)**
- Year-round mild (10–20°C spring/fall; 5–15°C winter)
- Best: March–May, September–November
- Rainy season: June–August (some areas)
- Winter: Cool but clear skies
- Most pleasant climate overall

### Holidays to Avoid

**Golden Week (Oct 1–7)**
- China's major holiday; entire country traveling
- All hotels booked, flights full, restaurants packed
- Prices inflate 20–50%
- Public toilets have lines
- **Recommendation:** Avoid if possible; stay outside China or hole up in apartment

**Chinese New Year (Lunar New Year)**
- Dates vary (Jan/Feb); 2 weeks off work
- Tier-1 cities empty (people return home)
- Some businesses closed
- Travel difficult; flights booked
- **Strategy:** Either embrace it (festive energy) or avoid

**Other Holidays (Single Days)**
- Qingming Festival (April; tomb-visiting)
- Dragon Boat Festival (June)
- Mid-Autumn Festival (September)
- Less crowded than Golden Week but still busy

## Great Firewall Survival Kit: Deep Dive

### Daily Workflow Behind the Firewall

**Reality Check**
- You'll spend 20–40% extra time on connectivity issues
- Some tools won't work reliably
- Expect VPN protocol blocks every 3–6 months (temporary 1–2 week outages)
- Build async workflow; don't rely on real-time sync

**Morning Setup Ritual**
1. Turn on VPN before touching work apps
2. Test Gmail, Google Drive, Slack (tell you if VPN is working)
3. If blocked: Switch to backup VPN; wait 5 minutes
4. Start work on local tools first (code editor, writing); sync later
5. Batch cloud operations (upload, download) in single sync window

**Workflow Patterns That Work**
- **Write locally, sync manually:** Use VS Code locally; git push when VPN stable
- **Async-first meetings:** Document everything; use recorded video instead of live calls
- **Batch email:** Check Gmail 2–3× daily instead of real-time
- **Local caching:** Keep offline copies of critical docs
- **Notification management:** Turn off real-time alerts (causes lag); manual refresh instead

---

### VPN Rotation Strategy

**Why Rotation Matters**
- Individual VPN protocols get detected and blocked (1–3 month cycles)
- Providers respond by updating; lags create gaps
- Rotating reduces fingerprinting

**Strategy**
1. Primary: ExpressVPN (Lightway protocol)
2. Secondary: NordVPN (Obfuscated protocol)
3. Tertiary: Mullvad (stealth mode)
4. If all three fail: Likely government-wide block (rare; temporary; wait 1–2 weeks)

**Setup**
- All three installed on laptop; all three on phone
- All three credentials written down (backup if app crashes)
- Test each monthly; note which works best
- Rotate primary every 2–3 months to avoid fingerprinting

---

### Data Backup Strategy (Can't Trust Cloud)

**Challenge**
- Google Drive / OneDrive / Dropbox require VPN (slow; unreliable)
- Chinese cloud (Baidu Cloud, Aliyun): Works fast; privacy concerns
- Solution: Local + USB backup

**Recommended Approach**
1. **Local machine:** Everything in ~/Work/
2. **USB backup (weekly):** Plug in external drive; rsync everything
3. **Cloud (monthly):** Upload to Google Drive (via VPN) as archives
4. **Redundancy:** If laptop dies, USB is 1-week backup; cloud is 1-month backup

**For Shared Documents**
- Download PDF versions daily (can't rely on real-time sync)
- Use offline mode in Google Docs (cumbersome but works)
- Prefer tools with offline mode (VS Code, Notion, Figma all have options)

## Final Honest Assessment

### China Is Right If You:
- Want lowest costs in East Asia (outside SEA)
- Are comfortable with complexity and friction
- Speak Mandarin or willing to learn
- Value scale and ambition (mega-cities push you)
- Are resilient to VPN drama and censorship
- Want to understand a future superpower

### China Is Wrong If You:
- Want seamless internet (Great Firewall is real)
- Speak no Mandarin and prefer English
- Like straightforward visa processes
- Value work-life balance (hustle culture intense)
- Want unrestricted access to Western tools/platforms
- Are building with Google Workspace, Slack, Notion as core tools

### The Goldilocks Option: Dali
- Cost: $800–1,000/month (unbeatable)
- Community: Established nomad hub (3+ years)
- Vibe: Relaxed, creative, intentional
- Tradeoff: Mandarin essential; internet variable
