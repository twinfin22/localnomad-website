# Anti-AI Checklist — Full Reference

Based on X-Poster original methodology. This is a **checklist pass/fail system**, NOT a scoring rubric.

Find ALL instances. Replace EVERY one. Zero tolerance.

### Chatbot Language Remnants (delete entirely)
- I hope this helps
- let me know if you have questions
- here is a detailed breakdown
- of course! / certainly! / you're absolutely right
- is there anything else / more detailed breakdown / here is a comprehensive

### Significance/Legacy Inflation (replace with specific claims)
- lasting impact → [state the specific effect]
- broader significance → [state what specifically changed]
- cultural legacy → [name the specific cultural thing]
- transformative impact → [state what transformed, with numbers]
- enduring influence → [name what was influenced, specifically]
- pivotal role in shaping → [state what was shaped and how]

### Vague Attribution Phrases (cite source or delete)
- scholars suggest / critics have noted / widely regarded as
- many believe / observers have pointed out / it is generally accepted / some experts argue

**Replacement strategy**: specific, short, opinionated, varied rhythm, concrete, repeat don't rotate, punctuate simply.

## Step 2: Banned Structures Scan

### 1. Rule-of-Three
"X, Y, and Z" rhetorical triads → pick the best one, drop the rest.

### 2. Negative Parallelisms
"Not just X but Y" / "Not merely X but Y" / "Not only X but also Y" / "More than just X" → assert directly.

### 3. Meta-Commentary
"In this article, we will explore..." / "Let's take a closer look at..." → delete entirely.

### 4. Excessive Transitions
"Furthermore," "Moreover," "Additionally," → vary or remove. Max 2 transition words per post.

### 5. Hollow Superlatives
"truly remarkable," "incredibly important" → be specific about WHY.

### 6. Fake Authority & Vague Attributions
"Experts agree," "Studies show," "Scholars suggest" (without citation) → cite or remove.

### 7. "In conclusion/summary"
Delete. Just write the closing thought.

### 8. Elegant Variation (Synonym Cycling)
Same entity called 3+ different names → reuse the original term. Max 2 natural alternatives.

### 9. Em Dash Overuse
Max 3 per 500 words. Replace excess with commas, parentheses, colons, periods.

### 10. Significance/Legacy/Impact Inflation
"lasting impact," "broader significance," "cultural legacy" → state the specific measurable effect.

### 11. "Challenges and Future Prospects" Pattern
"Despite its [positives], [subject] faces challenges..." → name specific problems directly.

### 12. Promotional/Advertisement-Like Language
Breathless enthusiasm, feature-listing, superlative stacking → report factually.

### 13. Chatbot Language Remnants
"I hope this helps," "Let me know," "Here is a detailed breakdown" → delete entirely.

---

## Step 3: Structural Checks

All 10 must pass:

- [ ] Section opening sentences have DIFFERENT structures
- [ ] Paragraph lengths VARY (mix of 1-sentence, 2-sentence, 3-4 sentence)
- [ ] First sentence of post is NOT a definition or general statement
- [ ] No "In conclusion," "In summary," "To sum up" anywhere
- [ ] H2 headers do NOT follow a repetitive pattern
- [ ] ≥3 informal/conversational elements (contractions, fragments, asides, direct address, rhetorical questions)
- [ ] ≥1 clear opinion or judgment expressed (not just neutral reporting)
- [ ] No synonym cycling: each key entity uses max 2 natural alternatives
- [ ] Em dash density: ≤3 per 500 words
- [ ] No "Despite... challenges" formulaic template

---

## Result

- ALL checks pass (0 banned words, 0 banned structures, all 10 structural checks green) = **PASS**
- ANY failure = **FAIL** → auto-rewrite failing elements → re-check
- Max 3 auto-fix attempts. After 3 rounds still failing, flag for Gen's manual review.
