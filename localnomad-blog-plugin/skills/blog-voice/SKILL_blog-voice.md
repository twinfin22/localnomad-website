# Blog Voice — LibaD-Adapted LocalNomad

## Priority Order (Conflict Resolution R7)

Voice and SEO coexist. When they conflict, voice wins — but keyword placement in headline, meta description, and slug is non-negotiable. First 100 words and subheading keyword placement are soft rules (include if natural, don't force).

See `_conflict-resolution.md` CONFLICT 7 for the full rationale.

## Voice DNA

**One-line summary**: A curious Korean nomad-builder who writes like she talks over soju — personal stories first, systemic observations second, self-deprecating humor always, footnotes as rabbit holes, and every metaphor drawn from the physical world she just walked through.

Read `references/voice-examples.md` for the full 8-section voice profile before writing any draft.

## How to Apply Voice

### 1. Sentence Architecture
- Alternate short fragments and flowing sentences. Never uniform length.
- Single-sentence paragraphs are encouraged (Fragment-as-Paragraph).
- Parenthetical asides for self-commentary, corrections, or humor.
- Example: "Nope." followed by "Colombians mean their greetings. Their hugs are thick, like a child throwing themselves into their mom's arms."

### 2. Humor & Self-Deprecation
- Self-mocking without victimhood: laugh at yourself, never play the victim.
- Deadpan observations: dry humor delivered flat, no setup-punchline.
- Korean-style (X) / (O) correction format for comic effect.
- Example: "Got mistaken for a waitress. Retired from networking on the spot."

### 3. Metaphor & Imagery
- Extended metaphors: people as animals/objects, sustained across paragraphs.
- Sensory-first writing: ground abstract ideas in physical sensations.
- Draw metaphors from the physical world just walked through.

### 4. Structural Patterns
- Numbered sections as mini-essays, NOT listicles.
- Footnotes as side quests: tangential but fascinating details go in footnotes.
- Sign-off catchphrase variation (enchovy school reference).
- Geographic structure when applicable.

### 5. Intellectual Texture
- Cross-cultural cross-pollination (East/West, past/present, tech/local).
- Multi-language sprinkling: Korean, Japanese, Chinese, etc. — ALWAYS with inline translation.
- Data as single punches, never in dense blocks.
- Intellectual references (Gramsci, nusantara etymology, etc.) are welcome in body — but keep sentences short and always explain context inline. Extended tangents → footnotes as side quests.

### 6. Emotional Register
- Earnest idealism + pragmatic skepticism: believe in big ideas, puncture hype immediately.
- Vulnerability without melodrama: share struggles matter-of-factly.
- Pivot from personal to systemic: anecdote → observation → action.

## ESL-Friendly Readability (Conflict Resolution R2)

Target audience: non-native English speakers (Korean/Japanese/Taiwanese nomads). Sentences simple, content rich.

1. **Sentences**: Short. One idea per sentence. LibaD already writes this way.
2. **Vocabulary**: High vocabulary is OK, but jargon/foreign terms get parenthetical explanation on first mention.
3. **Paragraphs**: 2-4 sentences. No 5+ sentence paragraphs.
4. **Subheadings**: Visual breathing room every 200-300 words (H2).
5. **Concepts**: Abstract ideas benefit from metaphor/analogy for accessibility.
6. **Footnotes**: Extended tangents go to footnotes (LibaD "side quests" pattern). Short intellectual references stay in body.

## Voice Anti-Patterns (NEVER)

- Never use: "delve," "crucial," "landscape," "tapestry," "vibrant," "navigate," "leverage" (corporate sense), "comprehensive guide"
- Never: corporate/polished/professional blogger tone
- Never: listicle-optimized structure
- Never: neutral — always have opinions, delivered casually
- Never: trying to sound smart — be smart but wear it lightly

## Link Preservation

When adjusting voice/tone of sentences containing inline markdown links `[text](url)`, preserve the link. Anchor text may change to match the new voice. URL must not be removed.

## CTA Tone

- Primary CTA (end of post): community tone, never salesy
  - BAD: "Subscribe now for weekly visa updates!"
  - GOOD: Community invitation or contextual link
- Inline CTA: contextual, woven into the narrative naturally
  - BAD: "Click here to compare visas"
  - GOOD: "E-7과 D-8 중 고민이라면 [비교 페이지]에서 나란히 볼 수 있다"
- Adopt: "specific about what happens next", "reduce risk"
- Reject: "create urgency", conversion-focused verbs
