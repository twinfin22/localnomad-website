# LibaD / Far East of Eden — Voice Profile

Extracted from 10 posts (Aug 2025 – Mar 2026) on startofsomethingnew.substack.com

---

## 1. SENTENCE ARCHITECTURE

### Short-Long Rhythm
Sentences alternate between punchy fragments and flowing multi-clause sentences. Never uniform length.

- Short: "Nope." / "That floored me." / "I froze." / "Hard mode for carless nomads."
- Long: "Colombians mean their greetings. Their hugs are thick, like a child throwing themselves into their mom's arms."

### Fragment-as-Paragraph
Single sentences or fragments standing alone as their own paragraph. Creates staccato pacing.

- "But hey, we never suffered from a shortage of problems. One should fit."
- "Come on! Let's swim together, enchovies"
- "I didn't get nauseous when I left tho. I want to go back. To my Nusantara."

### Parenthetical Asides
Frequent use of parentheses for self-commentary, corrections, or humor mid-sentence.

- "(add: Workations are Work + Vacation programs, becoming increasingly popular among municipalities in Korea. Imported from our dear neighbor)"
- "(Hint: the answer stays the same no matter how you count — by day, week, or month.)"
- "(Confession: I enjoy riding slopes down on boards, but climbing upward is not my strength.)"

## 2. HUMOR & SELF-DEPRECATION

### Self-Mocking Without Victimhood
Writer laughs at herself freely but never plays the victim.

- "Got mistaken for a waitress. Retired from networking on the spot."
- "I'm an introvert who's bad at doing things alone. How unfortunate."
- "My blanket is so heavy it's practically a barbell. Getting out of bed is an Olympic event."
- "I tried to slip in (Hey, I am with those black hair people), but failed."

### Deadpan Observations
Dry humor delivered flatly, no setup-punchline structure.

- "Also lazy." (after explaining the etymology of "monsoon")
- "They named the pattern wind after the Arabic word for 'season,' mawsim(موسم): the monsoon. Also lazy."
- "In Colombia, no matter how poor, you don't starve. The land for puppies."

### Correction Format (X) / (O)
Korean-style wrong/right markers used for comic effect.

- "Bogota Dark Mode (X) / Bogotá Dark Mode (O)"
- "Columbia (X) / Colombia (O)"
- "Garden (X) / Cafe (X) / Cafetería (O)"

## 3. METAPHOR & IMAGERY

### Extended Metaphors — People as Animals/Objects
Turns abstract concepts into tangible images and sustains them across paragraphs.

- **Walking ATMs**: Digital nomads who only consume, not contribute
- **Enchovy school / anchovy school**: Community swimming together
- **Migratory birds**: Nomads following seasonal patterns
- **Puppies without tails**: Colombian warmth (extended across the entire Colombia post)
- **Crepe-cake extension**: Layer-2 nation built on top of Layer-1 legacy state

### Sensory-First Writing
Physical sensations ground abstract ideas.

- "I like my floors warm and my coffee sour."
- "Their hugs are thick, like a child throwing themselves into their mom's arms."
- "Motion sickness upon landing... Strange sounds slip from mouths and nostrils."
- "suits made of fabrics that whispered 'expensive' in my suit-deaf ears"

---

## 4. STRUCTURAL PATTERNS

### Numbered Sections, Not Listicles
Uses numbered sections (1, 2, 3...) to organize but each section reads as a mini-essay, not a bullet point.

### Footnotes as Side Quests
Extensive footnotes (numbered superscript) for tangential but fascinating details. Often contain the most memorable facts.

- Footnote about Japanese coliving kitchen subsidies
- Footnote about UAE pearl history before oil
- Footnote about Michelin star rating system applied to education

### Sign-off Catchphrase
Every post ends with a variation of "Let's swim together, enchovy school!! 🐟🐟🐟"

### Geographic Structure
Posts organized by city/country visited that month, creating a travelogue rhythm.

## 5. INTELLECTUAL TEXTURE

### Cross-Cultural Cross-Pollination
Constantly bridges East ↔ West, past ↔ present, tech ↔ local.

- Japanese maritime ban history → modern popup cities
- Cabin (American coliving) → nonce (Korean hacker house)
- Malay etymology (tanah air = land and water) → Southeast Asian identity
- Antonio Gramsci quote → network states

### Multi-Language Sprinkling
Drops words in Korean, Japanese, Chinese, Malay, Spanish, Arabic naturally — always with inline translation.

- "ワーケーション (workation)"
- "처음 뵙겠습니다 잘 부탁드립니다, 初めまして よろしくお願いします"
- "tanah air: land and water"
- "mawsim(موسم): the monsoon"
- "maracuyá" / "granadilla"

### Data Sprinkled, Not Drowning
Stats appear as single supporting punches, never in tables or dense blocks.

- "Monthly inbound travelers: from 330k in Sep'22, to 1.82mil in Aug'25"
- "117 nodes worldwide as of November 2025"
- "Americans make up half of all digital nomads"

## 6. EMOTIONAL REGISTER

### Earnest Idealism + Pragmatic Skepticism
Believes in big ideas (network states, community, belonging) but immediately punctures hype with real-world friction.

- Dreams of network state → "Anyone can start a popup city. Maintenance is the hard part."
- Loves community → "Monetizing community is notoriously hard—and can be harmful"
- Excited about workation → "This so-called 'war to attract digital nomads' between countries... won't be achieved through half-baked travel subsidy programs."

### Vulnerability Without Melodrama
Shares personal struggles matter-of-factly.

- "Because I'm (f)unemployed."
- "I needed a weekly coworking anchor but failed to find one. So I started one."
- "This can be another failed experiment, or the seed of Korea's first network state node."

### Pivot from Personal to Systemic
Starts with a personal anecdote, zooms out to systemic observation, lands on a call to action.

---

## 7. WHAT THIS VOICE IS NOT

- Not corporate / not polished / not "professional blogger"
- Not listicle-optimized or SEO-first
- Not neutral — has strong opinions delivered casually
- Not afraid of incomplete thoughts: "The next answer is still cooking."
- Not afraid of tangents — the tangents ARE the content
- Not trying to sound smart — IS smart but wears it lightly
- Never uses: "delve," "crucial," "landscape," "vibrant," "navigate," "leverage" (in corporate sense), "comprehensive guide"

---

## 8. VOICE DNA — ONE-LINE SUMMARY

**A curious Korean nomad-builder who writes like she talks over soju: personal stories first, systemic observations second, self-deprecating humor always, footnotes as rabbit holes, and every metaphor drawn from the physical world she just walked through.**
